Roofing Replacement & Repair costs in Lehigh County — 2026

Lehigh County’s roofing market reflects its 83,411 owner-occupied households and a housing stock where 61.3% of homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024). This older inventory—especially in Center City Allentown—means many roofs here are nearing the end of their service life or were previously layered, which can affect replacement cost and scope. In South Whitehall and Upper Macungie, post‑World War II suburban tracts dominate. These neighborhoods were built en masse, using similar framing and standard roof pitches, so labor and materials are easier to estimate—a volume business of mid-sized, repeatable asphalt shingle jobs with few architectural surprises.

Because architectural asphalt shingles are so common here—cost-efficient for large, similar roof fields—they set the baseline for Lehigh County pricing. Metal standing seam and flat roofs are specialists’ work, typically installed on newer custom homes or flat sections over commercial blocks in Allentown and Bethlehem. Repairs ramp up sharply after wind events, especially near the Trexler Hills and South Mountain ridges, but the bulk of volume remains full roof replacements on single-family homes. Even with similar designs, prices in Lehigh County range widely due to house size, multiple shingle layers, and unknowns beneath the surface—making a detailed quote essential.

Why gutters get replaced with the roof

In Lehigh County, gutters, drip edge, and downspouts are best seen as one system moving water from your new roof away from your foundation. Replacing a roof and reusing failing gutters is tempting, but it usually leaves a weak link: old gutters can dump water too close to the house, saturating soils and leading to the basement seepage problems so common around Allentown and Bethlehem. Water getting behind poorly aligned old gutters can also rot new fascia and cause ice damming at the eaves—a frequent issue in local neighborhoods with high snow loads and deep frosts.

Many roofing warranties require that updated gutters and downspout discharge keep water at least several feet from the house. If drainage is incomplete, leaks in the new roof edge or pooling near the foundation could void coverage. Whenever we do a full shingle tear-off or replace decking on these older Lehigh Valley homes, it’s our standard practice to coordinate new pre-finished aluminum gutters and larger downspouts—paired with new drip edge—so the whole water management system works together.

Gutter replacement typically ranges from $2,000–$4,000 when bundled with a roof job, including solid aluminum guards if you have heavy tree cover. Choosing to replace gutters at the same time as the roof usually ends up more cost-effective than coming back later for a piecemeal fix.

Material comparison for Lehigh County homeowners

Lehigh County gets real snow: 40 pounds per square foot is the ground snow load, and design temperatures hit 4 °F most winters. The dominant roof choice here is architectural asphalt shingles, not just because they’re affordable, but because regional contractors are set up to install them expertly on neighborhood after neighborhood of similar homes. This shingle type strikes the best balance of longevity and cost for our housing stock, which is largely pitched at standard suburban slopes.

Metal standing seam is rarely the best dollar-for-dollar choice. It sheds snow better than anything else—think of those deep banks you see slide off a barn or new build—but installation cost is steep, starting at $24,000–$43,000. Most Lehigh County homeowners simply don’t recoup the extra upfront spend unless their home sits under trees where ice dams and freeze/thaw cycles are unmanageable with shingles. On the other end, TPO and EPDM membranes are specialty systems for flat roof sections that you’ll find on Allentown’s mixed-use buildings or the odd suburban low-slope addition.

Architectural shingles suit the region’s typical 25–30 year re-roof expectations. The only time to splurge on Class 4 impact-resistant shingles—priced up to $21,000 installed—is when your street gets regular hail or wind-driven debris off the South Mountain ridges. Otherwise, that extra cost doesn’t return value for most homes.

Storm damage and insurance claims in Lehigh County

After a wind or hail event in Lehigh County, document everything before anything is cleaned up. Take photos of the roof, detached shingles, and any interior leaks—even if the leak looks minor. Contact your insurance company and request a clear, written scope of loss. A qualified roofer should provide their own inspection and written estimate based on both visible and suspected damage. Make sure you understand whether your policy pays actual cash value (depreciated) or replacement cost—this difference can be thousands of dollars, especially on older Allentown homes.

Expect your insurer to send an adjuster; do not accept a handshake agreement or "ballpark" verbal deal from any party. In Pennsylvania, the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act (HICPA) requires a written contract for all home improvement work over $500—and you have a 3 business-day right of cancellation. Reputable roofers will never offer to cover or waive your insurance deductible. That practice is prohibited, and any contractor making such an offer is not operating within state law. It’s a red flag: steer clear.

Always get a signed proposal matching the insurance scope, and never pay out of pocket in excess of your deductible unless you’ve specifically approved upgrades (like Class 4 shingles at $16,500–$21,000). After repairs, keep all receipts in case there’s an insurer follow-up. Lehigh County’s clustered storm damage—especially in the western townships with funneled winds—can slow scheduling, so file promptly and be prepared for roofers and adjusters to get busier during peak events.

Roofing permits in Lehigh County: what you need before work starts

Lehigh County follows the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), based on the International Residential Code. The PA Department of Labor & Industry oversees the code, but enforcement is handled by each municipality—like the Bureau of Building Standards & Safety in Allentown, or separate offices in the surrounding townships.

In Allentown, a building permit is typically required for any structural roof replacement or when new roofing is installed over existing layers. Minor repairs—such as replacing a few damaged shingles—are often performed without a permit. Always confirm this with your local code office before scheduling work, especially if your home is in Allentown, Bethlehem, or the Macungie townships.

To protect yourself, check that your roofer has Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration—required for contracts over $500. Verify the HIC number at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. For workers' compensation coverage, which is mandatory for all employers in Pennsylvania, check the contractor’s compliance via the state WCAIS database at wcais.pa.gov. Written contracts are required by law for roof work over $500, and you have a three-business-day right to cancel any signed agreement.

What moves the price of a roof in Lehigh County

Roof replacement quotes diverge sharply here because of three things: the age of our housing—over 61.3% built before 1980—multiple existing shingle layers, and Lehigh County’s storm exposure, especially in western townships near South Mountain and Trexler Hills. Many Allentown and Macungie roofs have been “roofed over” once or twice, so a new job often requires full tear-off and dumpster fees on top of new shingles. With a typical architectural shingle roof running $10,000–$21,000, the number of layers and the condition of underlying decking can quickly move your quote within that range.

Snow load also plays a part: our 40 psf ground snow ratings and severe ice dam potential mean that underlayment and ice barrier (sometimes up to a 3-foot width at the eaves) are a must for code compliance and lasting performance. Steep-pitched homes—common in Center City Allentown’s older stock—add labor hours and harness costs, while sprawling suburban roofs in Whitehall get priced by “squares” but are easier to stage.

Material upgrades (like jumping from architectural to Class 4 impact-resistant shingles) add between $500–$2,500 per roof depending on size. And when hidden decking rot surfaces, expect an add-on of $75–$150 per plywood sheet replaced—something that’s almost always an unknown until the old roof is off.

How to compare roofing quotes in Lehigh County

A good roofing estimate in Lehigh County spells out details: the total roof area measured in “squares” (100 square feet each), the exact pitch, and whether one or more old shingle layers are being torn off. It should itemize dumpster and disposal charges, specify the underlayment and ice barrier brands, and give a per-sheet price in case hidden plywood decking needs replacement. You also want to see line items for new flashing, vent boots, and exactly what both the workmanship and manufacturer’s warranties cover—those should never be lumped together.

A vague or “bundled” quote usually hides the most expensive surprises. If you’re comparing roofers, and only one gives you the roof size and how many tear-off layers are included, their price is the only one you can actually hold accountable. Payment schedules should require no more than 15% up front, with the rest only after you’re satisfied, and everything—especially product types—deserves to be in writing.

Timing a roof replacement in Lehigh County

Lehigh County’s roofing season runs from late March through November, with asphalt shingle installations best performed above 45°F so sealing strips can bond properly. Most shingle jobs are scheduled for late spring and early fall—after the thaw and before the first hard frosts—because temperatures are optimal and ventilation is easier to inspect as part of the work.

August and September are the busiest months for reputable roofers in Allentown and Bethlehem, thanks to a dense local contractor market and a rush to button up before winter. This means lead times can stretch to three or even four weeks out. Try to schedule early to beat that Labor Day rush. Emergency tarping and winter repairs—especially after February ice storms or heavy wet snow—cost more, both for materials and overtime. Expect repair rates on leak calls to be higher from December through March, and be aware that full replacements mid-winter often carry manufacturer limitations on warranty due to cold weather application.

For most Lehigh County homes, your least expensive and least stressful option is planning a full roof replacement for mid-to-late summer. If you’re waiting for storm season hoping for hail coverage, know that clusters of insurance claims will also tie up contractor crews—so prompt action after any weather event is worth it for your peace of mind.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in Lehigh County

Do metal roofs shed snow better in Lehigh County?

Yes, standing seam metal roofs shed snow and ice much more effectively than shingles in Lehigh County’s climate. With the county’s 40 psf ground snow load and severe ice dam exposure (especially near South Mountain), the smooth surface of a metal roof allows snow sheets to slide off before meltwater can refreeze at the eaves. This minimizes leaks from ice dams, which are a recurring winter problem in older Allentown and Bethlehem homes. However, installing standing seam metal runs $24,000–$43,000—meaning it’s best suited to new builds or homes with ongoing ice dam problems. Most homeowners stick to architectural shingles unless ice dams or repeated snow buildup have been causing costly leaks or soffit damage each winter.

How do I find a reputable roofer in Lehigh County?

Start by verifying the roofer’s Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration—every roofer working on contracts over $500 must have one, and you can check their number on hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Ask for proof of workers’ compensation insurance, which you can also check via WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov). Look for estimates that state the number of squares, shingles by brand, and all tear-off and decking contingencies. In Lehigh County’s saturated roofing market (Allentown–Bethlehem), there are a lot of outfits, but not all are equal. Don’t be swayed by a low bid for a typical architectural shingle replacement (should be $10,000–$21,000), especially if the quote is vague. It’s also a requirement in Pennsylvania that you receive a written contract and a 3-day right to cancel over $500—don’t skip that step.

What if my insurer denies my roof claim?

If your roof insurance claim is denied in Lehigh County, ask for a written explanation of the specific exclusion. Many denials occur because the damage is considered “wear and tear” or pre-existing, which is a real risk given that 61.3% of homes here were built before 1980. If you disagree with the decision, obtain a second opinion from a roofer who works regularly with insurance scopes—some issues, like wind-blown shingle creases, do require experienced documentation. You’re still responsible for repairs; a typical localized roof repair will run $550–$2,000 depending on section size. If the insurer’s denial seems arbitrary, you may consider mediation through the PA Insurance Department, but avoid paying anyone to “fight” your claim—reputable roofers will help you document legitimate losses as part of their service.

Do I need to leave my house during a roof replacement?

In most Lehigh County homes, you don’t need to leave during a standard roof replacement, even for a complete tear-off job. Installers can start and finish a mid-sized single-family architectural shingle roof (at $10,000–$21,000) in just one to two days. There will be noise, and ceiling fixtures may vibrate a bit, but you can safely remain inside, just avoid working directly below gutters and entrances while debris is being tossed. If you have pets sensitive to noise, or if major decking or structural work is needed (common in older Allentown homes), a day’s outing may help. Crews will tarp landscaping and clean up nails, but a quick post-completion sweep is always smart.

If you need emergency repairs, like a tarp, keep all receipts. Your roofer’s estimate should match the insurer’s scope; don’t authorize more work without approval. For a typical small repair, plan on $550–$2,000. If a roofer offers to pay or “waive” your deductible, walk away—this is illegal in Pennsylvania. Filing promptly after storms, especially where funneled winds hit the west side, helps you secure a repair slot before the area’s contractors get swamped.

Why do roofs fail faster in northeastern Pennsylvania?

Roofs in northeastern Pennsylvania, including Lehigh County, experience quicker wear due to harsh winters, freeze-thaw cycles, high snow loads of 40 psf, and summer hail or wind squalls that strike older housing stock. Ice dams are notorious in Allentown and Bethlehem, attacking the lower roof edges and working water under shingles. Many postwar homes had original ventilation and insulation that doesn’t meet today’s standards, leading to attic heat loss and rapid snow melt/refreeze. Even a well-installed architectural shingle roof, costing $10,000–$21,000, may need earlier replacement in these conditions unless proper attic ventilation and ice barriers are added during installation. Regular inspections after winter storms are the best way to catch small leaks before they cause interior ceiling damage.

Is a sagging roof line an emergency?

A sagging roof in Lehigh County should be treated as a priority, especially given the prevalence of homes built before 1980 where framing and decking may not meet newer standards. While it may not collapse immediately, sagging indicates possible structural failure—even more so after heavy snow loads or ice buildup common in this region. Get a roofer or structural contractor to inspect and document the issue right away. Emergency tarping may run $550–$2,000 if you have active leaks, but you’ll want a full assessment of rafters and decking as soon as possible. Insurance may help if collapse is imminent or damage is storm-related. Don’t walk on or under the sagging section, and avoid cheap patch jobs; safety and structure come first in Lehigh County’s climate.

What's the cheapest way to replace a roof in Lehigh County?

The lowest-cost full replacement in Lehigh County is a single-layer tear-off followed by standard architectural shingles, typically quoted in the $10,000–$13,500 range for smaller homes with no decking issues. Don’t be tempted by a “roof-over” (installing new over old shingles)—it often voids warranties and sets up more cost down the road, especially with our severe winter freeze/thaw cycles. For homes with simple gables and easy access in places like South Whitehall, you may come in at the low end of this range, but beware of any quote far below: it might skip proper underlayment or ice barrier, which are critical for performance here. Saving money by forgoing gutters or skipping tear-off is penny wise, pound foolish in Lehigh County’s climate.

What does roof replacement cost per square in Lehigh County?

In Lehigh County, most reputable asphalt architectural shingle replacements run between $550–$750 per “square” (100 square feet), with most roofs totaling $10,000–$21,000 installed depending on home size, pitch, and tear-off work. The per-square number includes disposal, labor, underlayment, new drip edge, and basic flashing. If you’re quoted much lower per square, check if it omits decking or ice & water shield—those can add up fast when revealed during tear-off. Upgrading to Class 4 impact-resistant shingles bumps that number to nearly $800–$950 per square. Metal and flat membrane roofs cost more, sometimes double. Always ask for the square count to back up the lump sum total.

Is ice barrier underlayment required in Lehigh County?

Yes, Lehigh County follows the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), which, based on the IRC, requires an ice barrier (ice and water shield) at the eaves in any area prone to snow and ice damming—which describes almost all of northeastern Pennsylvania, including Allentown and the surrounding townships. Installers apply a self-adhered membrane typically 3 feet up from the roof edge, or 2 feet beyond the interior wall. The cost is usually folded into a standard quote but comes to $300–$800 extra for a typical home. Omitting the ice barrier frequently voids manufacturer warranties and puts you at risk in severe winters. If a quote leaves this item out or calls it “optional,” be skeptical—this is not the place to cut corners in Lehigh County.

Are there historic district rules affecting roofing in Lehigh County?

Yes, certain parts of Allentown—especially Center City around Hamilton Street—have historic guidelines or façade review for roofing in historic districts. While the rest of Lehigh County is less regulated, if your property is in a designated preservation area, your choice of shingle type, color, or visible flashing may be subject to architectural review before a building permit is issued. This can affect timeline and materials used; your contractor should confirm with Allentown’s Bureau of Building Standards & Safety if you’re unsure. Roofing costs aren’t directly higher but delays for approval can stretch the process. For most South Whitehall and Macungie tract homes, these rules do not apply; expect a standard asphalt shingle replacement to run $10,000–$21,000 with no extra steps needed.
