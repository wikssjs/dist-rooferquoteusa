Workmanship vs manufacturer warranty

Greensburg homeowners often confuse the two warranties that come with a new roof: workmanship and manufacturer. They protect you in different ways, and knowing the difference matters at resale and when issues come up down the road.

The manufacturer warranty covers defects in the roofing material itself—shingles, underlayment, or membrane. Most are prorated, meaning coverage drops over time, and registration deadlines usually apply within 60–90 days of installation. If you move, these warranties are often transferable to one buyer (sometimes for a fee), an important point for homeowners planning to sell.

A workmanship warranty, on the other hand, is the promise from your chosen installer to stand behind their labor. It covers problems like poor flashing, bad fastener placement, or incorrect ventilation—errors that won’t show up until the first hard freeze or heavy rain. Length varies widely: some national outfits throw out 25-year claims, but if the contractor has only been around for two years, that paper isn’t worth much. In Greensburg, ten solid years from a company that’s survived past cycles means more than a lifetime promise from a truck with new magnets on the door.

Both warranties can be voided if attic vents are blocked by insulation, if your installer lays over old shingles, or if you ignore local code. Register your product, ask for specifics about transfer, and get both warranties in writing in your HICPA-compliant contract.

Pennsylvania code and Westmoreland County permit rules for roofing

In Greensburg and the surrounding Westmoreland County townships, roofing projects fall under the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), which follows the International Residential Code. Permitting is managed by the City of Greensburg Planning and Development Department for city jobs, and by each township’s own code enforcement office for outlying areas. If a township opts out of enforcement, the PA Department of Labor & Industry steps in.

Most full roof replacements or structural decking repairs require a permit, while minor repairs—like replacing a few shingles or a single small leak—often do not (but always check with the local office). When hiring a roofer for any project over $500, the contractor must be registered as a Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC). You can check their registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov and ensure their insurance is current.

Pennsylvania requires all employers to carry workers’ compensation insurance, which you can confirm through the WCAIS system at wcais.pa.gov. Don’t skip these checks: a reputable Greensburg roofer will provide proof of both on request. For homes built before 1978, federal EPA Lead RRP rules apply—look up their certification if your home is in that era.

Timing a roof replacement in Greensburg

Southwestern Pennsylvania’s roofing calendar is shaped by cold, wet winters and humid, stormy summers. The best installation window in Greensburg runs from late April through early November, when shingle adhesives seal properly and crews are at full strength. Early booking is crucial here—moderate suburban market density, with crews serving neighborhoods all over the greater Pittsburgh metro, means that scheduling can jam up, especially as fall approaches and everyone rushes to finish before the first hard freeze.

If an emergency hits in mid-winter, such as a leaking roof in February, expect costs at the upper end of the repair range. Crews must battle snow, ice, and short daylight hours, which means higher labor costs and higher material waste. On the other hand, a planned replacement scheduled for September or October will not only save you stress but can land you the best selection of local installers and fairer pricing.

Heavy spring rains can sometimes mean short delays for tear-offs, but contractors here are used to working around weather patterns unique to Greensburg’s hills and valleys. If you know your roof is near the end, don’t wait until it leaks—get on the schedule by summer to avoid both premium winter rates and the last-minute fall rush.

Flat roof sections: the part most quotes get wrong

In Greensburg and throughout Westmoreland County, many older homes have a flat or nearly flat roof section—think porch covers, back additions, or even the occasional rowhouse rear. These areas should not be roofed with standard asphalt shingles. When a contractor quotes shingles for a section under 3/12 pitch (especially anything at 2/12), it’s a clear sign they’re ignoring building science and local code. Shingles depend on gravity and pitch for drainage, and on flat surfaces, water sits, finds seams, and quickly leaks.

The proper solution for Greensburg’s flat sections is a single-ply membrane (TPO or EPDM). These systems last 15–25 years here—roughly half the average shingle lifespan—but they are dramatically more reliable than “stretching” shingle coverage. Expect a replacement of a flat roof membrane to run $11,000–$22,000 for a typical home section. Proper drainage is essential: ponding water combined with our freeze-thaw cycles will kill a flat roof fast, leading to leaks and hidden rot.

A flat section quoted in shingles is a shortcut. Make sure membrane, insulation, and drainage are called out specifically on your contract.

The scope items Greensburg homeowners don't budget for

Westmoreland County crews know that roof replacement here often uncovers more than cracked shingles. With so many Greensburg homes built before 1980, hidden scope is almost guaranteed once tear-off begins.

  • Rotted decking: Once the old roof’s up, expect to find sections of rotted or delaminating wood, especially near gutters and valleys. Replacing a typical sheet of decking in Greensburg runs $150–$250 per sheet, with most jobs needing 2–6 sheets replaced on older homes.
  • Attic ventilation: Poor ventilation is a chronic issue here, compounding ice dams during freeze-thaw and voiding shingle warranties. Upgrading to code—a ridge vent and proper soffit intake—costs $700–$1,500 but protects your investment. This isn't just efficiency: inadequate airflow leads to ice dams, causing leaks and premature shingle failure, especially in our 8 °F winter design temps.
  • Flashing and vent boots: Original flashing and boots around chimneys, vents, and walls are often rusted or missing. Replacements typically run $200–$500 for the full roof, but missed flashing is a leading cause of “unexpected” leaks post-install.
  • Drip edge: Many old roofs never had proper drip edge installed. Local code now requires it, and it usually adds $120–$300 to a job.

Always ask your contractor if these items are included in your estimate. Too many Greensburg homeowners are hit with extras after the fact—budgeting for them up front avoids sticker shock.

When a Greensburg roof is worth repairing — and when it isn't

Deciding between repair and replacement on a Greensburg roof is about age, leak pattern, and what’s under the shingles. For houses built in the 1960s–1980s boom, most original roofs are long past replacement age, and repairs on 25+ year materials rarely buy much time. If your roof is under 15 years old and there’s just one leak—say, a bad flashing or a cracked boot—a targeted repair makes sense. At the repair price point of $450–$1,750, you’re not going wrong.

When leaks multiply, or when a roof is well into its second decade, repeated repairs easily outpace the cost of new shingles. Two or three service calls at $1,100 each quickly approach $3,300—that’s a third of a full replacement, but with no long-term guarantee.

Severe storm damage or active leaks going into winter are never jobs to kick down the road. Greensburg’s freeze-thaw cycles mean water that gets in now will do exponentially more damage by spring.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in Greensburg

What causes ice dams on Greensburg roofs? Ice dams in Greensburg are mainly due to our heavy freeze-thaw cycles, especially in older homes where attic ventilation is often insufficient. When warm air escapes into the attic, it melts snow on the roof. Water runs down your shingles until it hits the cold eaves, where it refreezes and creates a “dam.” Water then backs up under shingles, leading to leaks. The city’s 8°F winter design temperatures make this worse, and most insulation and venting in homes built before 1980 aren’t up to modern code. Upgrading attic vents and adding a properly installed ice and water shield—especially when doing a full replacement priced at $8,700–$18,500—can save extensive water damage repairs down the road.
How much does it cost to repair a roof leak in Greensburg? Repairing a roof leak in Greensburg typically ranges from $450–$1,750 depending on severity and location. Most single-leak jobs—like flashing or vent boot replacements—fall closer to the lower end, especially if caught early. If interior damage is present or if the roof is especially steep or hard to access, you may be at the upper end. Keep in mind that “just a leak” often uncovers hidden damage, like rotted decking or failing underlayment, especially in the area’s older housing stock. It’s usually best to fix a leak quickly to avoid even higher costs from interior repairs or mold remediation in a Greensburg winter.
How do I know if my Greensburg roof needs replacing? Look for curled, cracked, or missing shingles, exposed nails, granule loss in gutters, or leaks that reappear after repairs. Homes in Greensburg built before 1980 are likely to have had at least one roof replacement, but if yours is over 20 years old, or if multiple repairs no longer hold, it’s time for a conversation about new shingles. When repairs at $1,100 a pop start adding up, or if you see daylight through the attic decking, don’t wait. Winter damage and insurance eligibility hinge on prompt action here.
Is ice barrier underlayment required in Greensburg? Yes, Greensburg projects are covered by the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code, which references the IRC’s requirement for ice barrier (ice and water shield) along eaves and valleys in cold climates. If your contractor isn’t including it by default, consider it a red flag. A full roof replacement, typically running $8,700–$18,500, should always include high-quality ice and water shield, especially given the historic freeze-thaw swings here.
Can a contractor cover my insurance deductible in Pennsylvania? No, under the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act (HICPA), it’s illegal for any contractor working in Greensburg or anywhere in Pennsylvania to waive or “absorb” your homeowner’s insurance deductible. Anyone promising otherwise is noncompliant and risks voiding your claims. Legitimate roof replacements after storm damage are routinely covered by insurance, but you, the homeowner, are responsible for your policy deductible, separate from the project cost—often a percentage of the total, which is commonly $1,000 or more for full roof claims.
What happens if it rains mid-replacement? Experienced Greensburg crews follow weather forecasts closely and only tear off what they can re-cover the same day. If rain is likely, reputable contractors will “dry-in” the roof with waterproof underlayment, and emergency tarps are always on-hand. Your contract should spell this out, but you should never see open decking left exposed overnight. If bad weather causes delays, costs don’t typically increase unless water gets into the home, and a basic tarp response falls under the repair range of $450–$1,750. Always ask how weather contingencies are handled before work begins.
How much does a flat roof replacement cost in Greensburg? Flat roof replacement using TPO or EPDM membrane in Greensburg usually costs $11,000–$22,000 for a residential section such as a porch, addition, or garage. Factors pushing cost higher include complicated tie-ins to old masonry, extensive decking rot, or the need to re-do perimeter flashing. Most older homes in this area, especially those with additions dating from the 1960s or 1970s, require careful consideration of drainage and ponding, given the clay subgrade and freeze-thaw cycles typical of Westmoreland County.
Should I use a local roofer or a national company? In Greensburg and the rest of Westmoreland County, local roofing contractors are usually a stronger bet than out-of-town or national chains. Local crews understand our region’s heavy snow load (30 psf), the freeze-thaw cycles, and the quirks of homes built pre-1980. After major hail events, you’ll see national storm chasers dropping flyers, but local companies have to honor their $8,700–$18,500 warranties, maintain reputation, and answer your call if something goes wrong in January. Always check for HIC registration, proper insurance, and referrals within the Greensburg community.
How long do architectural shingles last compared to 3-tab? Architectural shingles, now the Greensburg standard for replacements, have an average lifespan of 25–30 years in our climate versus 15–20 years for old-style 3-tabs, which are nearly phased out by local code and manufacturer supply. The higher wind resistance and thicker profile of architectural asphalt pay for themselves over time, especially with our Ohio Valley storm patterns. The cost difference up front—typically $8,700–$18,500 for architectural—means much longer service before the next replacement, and a better warranty for issues like wind or ridge-line damage common in Westmoreland County.
