Because Dauphin County is part of the Harrisburg–Carlisle metro with a saturated roofer market, last-minute bookings in peak season are tough—roofers can fill their September calendars by midsummer. Reading a roofing estimate line by line

South Hanover homeowners get the best deal when they know exactly what’s in their roofing estimate. An accurate quote spells out the roof area in “squares” (each equals 100 sq ft), with the pitch (steepness) noted—this ensures that you’re not overpaying or getting an apples-to-oranges comparison. Look for line items: every layer of shingle to be torn off (and the cost of the dumpster), specific underlayment products (like synthetic or ice barrier), and how much is included. Decking replacement (the wood under your shingles) should be priced per sheet as a contingency line—if rot or water damage is found, you don’t want a surprise markup. Flashings around chimneys, vents, and valleys need to be spelled out, as do new vent boots. Warranty details should list the manufacturer’s product warranty separately from the labor or workmanship period offered by the contractor. Lastly, payment schedule matters: standard is a modest deposit—never large upfront sums—with the balance only due upon full completion.

A lump-sum quote with no square count, product names, or warranty details can hide poor value or future add-ons. Don’t be afraid to ask for specifics or to walk away from an estimate that “bundles” everything into a single figure—it protects the roofer, not you.

Checking out a roofer in South Hanover: what to verify

In South Hanover, you want more than a magnet on the fridge before you let anyone on your roof. Pennsylvania does not require a trade license for roofing, but every contractor must hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration from the PA Office of Attorney General if your contract is over $500—which is nearly always the case with roof work here. Verify the HIC number directly at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Workers’ compensation insurance is mandatory for all roofing employers—check status at WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov). Since most homes here were built before 1980, be alert to lead paint rules; federal EPA Lead RRP Rule applies if disturbing painted surfaces on pre-1978 houses (certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov). A legitimate roofer should provide a written contract (required by the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act), and waiving your insurance deductible isn’t legal.

  • Check HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov
  • Verify active workers’ compensation at WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov)
  • Demand certificate of liability insurance
  • Ask for EPA Lead RRP certification for pre-1978 homes
  • Require a written contract for any job over $500
  • Review contract for the 3-day right of cancellation
  • Ask for references from actual South Hanover or Dauphin County jobs
  • Review physical address and verify it's local—not just a PO box or suite
  • Confirm warranty details in writing (material and workmanship)
  • See proof of dumpster/rental agreement if roofing replacement
  • Do not pay cash for full deposit—use a check or credit for traceability
  • Never sign a blank or price-less contract

Red flags in South Hanover and the Harrisburg–Carlisle metro: door-knocking after storms, high-pressure sales, no local references, refusal to share HIC or insurance documents, and phone numbers that trace to out-of-area codes. In a saturated market, legitimate roofers win on clarity, not urgency.

Permits and code requirements in South Hanover

Roofing work in South Hanover is governed by the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), based on the IRC. Enforcement falls to municipalities or the PA Department of Labor & Industry if the township has opted out. For most full roof replacements or structural changes, a permit is required; basic repairs or single shingle swaps typically don't need one, but anything that affects the overall roof system (decking, structural work, or extensive re-roofing) does. Issuance and inspection are handled by the local South Hanover code office or, in some cases, by Labor & Industry directly if local enforcement is not in place.

Before any contract is signed, verify the roofer’s Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov, and confirm active workers' compensation insurance through the WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). The law requires workers' comp for all roofing employers. If your home was built before 1978 and painted surfaces will be disturbed during roof work, check that your roofer holds EPA Lead RRP certification. Make sure your contract is in writing, as required by the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act for jobs over $500, and beware of anyone offering to “take care of the permit”—permits are your protection, and the named contractor should always be the one registered and insured to do the work.

Roofing warranties in South Hanover: what's actually covered

Roofing warranties in South Hanover come in two separate tiers: the manufacturer warranty and the workmanship warranty. The manufacturer's warranty—prorated after the first decade—covers defects in the shingles or membrane material but almost never installation errors, and claim payment drops off over time rather than holding steady. Transferability at resale is common but not universal; most require timely registration by the new owner, usually within 30–60 days of purchase. Coverage is usually voided by improper ventilation, installation over old shingles, or poor roof maintenance. The workmanship warranty is offered by the company that installs the roof, covering leaks or failures from improper labor for a set time—frequently 5 to 10 years from reputable, established firms here; some offer more, but a “lifetime” or 25-year term from a short-lived or out-of-town operation is meaningless if they close up shop. In short, check both warranty documents and keep them with your home records. If your re-roof contract includes roof ventilation or new decking, be sure it is installed to code, or the warranty—even from the best manufacturer—won’t be honored.

Water management on a South Hanover roof

In South Hanover, a proper roof job isn’t complete without tying in gutters, downspouts, and ground drainage. Minor missed details here—that last downspout elbow or a missing drip edge—account for a surprising share of basement leaks in this township. Replacing the roof but leaving undersized, clogged, or leaky gutters is a common shortcut that just pushes the next water problem onto your foundation. With our rolling lawns and freeze-thaw cycles, you don’t want gutter discharge within a few feet of the house—move it at least 4–6 ft away. Most roofing warranties require that gutters are in good condition to prevent backflow and water intrusion at the eaves; neglect may void coverage if a future leak develops.

For cost: full gutter and downspout replacement on a typical house, bundled with a roof replacement, runs $2,000–$5,000 installed. Add gutter guards, and the range is $3,200–$7,500, depending on the guard type and total length. Make sure your proposal spells out seamless gutter gauge, downspout locations, and any grading work to direct water away from the house. Ignoring drainage in South Hanover just isn’t worth the headache—or the cost.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in South Hanover

Does attic ventilation matter in South Hanover's climate? Attic ventilation is important in South Hanover. Due to pronounced freeze-thaw cycling and a winter design temperature of 12 °F, inadequate ventilation can let moist air collect under the roof decking, leading to mold, rot, and shortened shingle lifespan. Modern codes in Pennsylvania require balanced intake and exhaust at eaves and ridges, promoting airflow and protecting both the insulation and the roof itself. Manufacturers may deny warranty claims if the roof system isn’t properly vented, especially for high-end products in the $10,500–$22,000 range. Good ventilation will also improve your home's energy performance during our 5,200 annual heating degree days, lowering the risk of ice dams and trapped heat in the summer. If your roofer doesn’t mention checking or upgrading ventilation, get another opinion—you’ll get better longevity and fewer headaches.
Can I install new shingles over old ones in Pennsylvania? In Pennsylvania, the Uniform Construction Code allows a single layer of new shingles over an existing one—if the current roof deck is sound and there’s no more than one prior layer. While some South Hanover homeowners look to save money by skipping a tear-off, this shortcut often ends up more costly. Layering traps moisture and heat, especially in the freeze-thaw cycles we get locally, and makes leak detection much harder. Manufacturer warranties for architectural shingles in the $10,500–$22,000 band are usually voided by layering. Roofers here will advise against it unless your decking and old roof are truly pristine. Plus, realtors see a resale discount when buyers spot layered shingles—no one wants a mystery roof history. If your home was built before 1978, also be aware that the EPA Lead RRP Rule applies if any painted surfaces are disturbed during roofing.

What is the difference between TPO and EPDM? Both TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) and EPDM (ethylene propylene diene monomer) are flat roof membranes used on South Hanover additions, porches, and the few mid-century low-slope builds in the neighborhood. TPO is a bright white, heat-welded membrane, reflecting more sun and running slightly cooler, making it good for energy savings during our 1,250 cooling degree days. EPDM is a black, rubber-like sheet, typically glued down, and offers proven long-term performance, especially in Pennsylvania's cold winters. Cost-wise, the price range for a flat roof replacement is $13,000–$26,000, with some premium options and details tilting closer to TPO. Both materials last 15–25 years if installed right. For South Hanover, the better option depends on the house orientation, budget, and installer comfort—both work well here when detailed flashing and drainage are done right.

Upgrading to impact-resistant shingles is in the $17,000–$22,000 band, and metal roofing costs jump to $25,000–$44,500. Replacement of a TPO or EPDM flat roof is $13,000–$26,000. These prices reflect the specific conditions and housing stock found in South Hanover: most jobs require a full tear-off, detailed flashing and ice guard (due to our freeze-thaw cycles), and everything above the state minimum in terms of labor and materials. How long does a roof replacement take in South Hanover? For the average-sized South Hanover single-family home, a full asphalt shingle roof replacement usually takes 1 to 3 days, assuming decent weather and no surprises with decking or structural issues. Add a day or two for metal installation or if your roof has a steep pitch, valleys, or multiple dormers. Flat roofs (TPO or EPDM) on additions or porches generally fit the same timeline—plus dry-in time if weather interrupts. In peak roofing season (September or June), crews are busy with clustered neighborhood jobs, so scheduling two to four weeks out is normal. If your job scope includes gutter replacement—running $2,000–$5,000 with a roof—budget an extra half-day to day for those crews. For February emergencies, expect extra time for snow removal and potential weather delays. Always ask your roofer about weather delays and how they’ll handle tarping if work is interrupted.
Do I need my own inspection before the adjuster visits? It’s not required for South Hanover homeowners to hire an independent inspector before your insurance adjuster visits for a storm or leak claim, but it’s often worth it. Having a reputable local roofing contractor walk the roof beforehand can help identify all legitimate storm or hail damage—especially at the flashing and ridge lines, which take a beating from wind off the river valley. Some issues, like lifted shingle edges and minor leaks, are easily missed by adjusters not familiar with South Hanover’s common problems. If you go this route, expect to pay for a formal evaluation (often credited if you proceed with repair) in the $550–$2,100 range. It could save a claim denial or keep you from settling for less than the true scope needed for a durable repair.
How much deposit should I pay a roofer upfront? In South Hanover and across Pennsylvania, reputable roofing contractors typically require a modest deposit—with 10% to 15% of the total contract amount standard for a full replacement. For a $17,000 roof, that means about $1,700–$2,550 upfront. There’s no legal maximum in Pennsylvania, but the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act strongly discourages large cash down payments. You should never be asked to pay the whole amount in advance, and for storm work, be extra cautious around any roofer requesting a big deposit before materials hit your driveway. Final payment is always due after the project is completed and you’ve had a chance to inspect the work. Protect yourself by paying with check or credit, not cash, and never to an individual—only to the registered company.
What are the signs of hail damage on a roof? In South Hanover, hail storms may be less common than wind, but when they hit, damage can show up as granule loss, “bruising” or soft spots on shingles, and cracked or dented flashing. Edges and ridge caps facing the Susquehanna River valley take a beating, and impact-resistant shingles ($17,000–$22,000) can make a real difference for homes on exposed hills. After a hail event, check for shingle discoloration, exposed fiberglass, or the presence of granules in the gutters and at downspout discharge points. Flashing and vent boots can show impact dents or splits. Even when leaks don’t appear right away, minor hail bruises can shorten the overall shingle lifespan or invalidate the manufacturer warranty if not addressed. Never go up yourself—have a trusted local roofer inspect after any severe hail in the area.
