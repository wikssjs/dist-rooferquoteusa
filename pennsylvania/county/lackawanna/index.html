Lackawanna County roofing prices by material and scope — 2026

Lackawanna County's roofing costs reflect both its older building stock and a competitive market shaped by the Scranton–Wilkes-Barre metro. With 73.0% of area homes built before 1980 and the majority—68.2%—comprised of single-family houses (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), most jobs here involve steep roofs, multiple past layers, and the hidden challenges that come with aged wood framing. Old Forge and Scranton, in particular, are filled with early-1900s wood-frame row and duplex homes. These structures routinely suffer from rotted sheathing and structural quirks that aren't obvious until a tear-off, making deck repairs a common part of any replacement job in Lackawanna County.

Repeated replacement demand—especially in West Side Scranton—means prices here typically run higher than online averages for other markets. It's not uncommon for a “basic” roof replacement to expand into decking and ventilation work once the old materials come off. Flat roofs are also a part of the landscape, especially on porches and rear additions, and they require different materials and pricing.

What a proper Lackawanna County roofing quote contains

Every legitimate roofing quote in Lackawanna County should detail specific measurements and materials. Make sure the quote names the exact square footage (“squares”) and roof pitch, not just a flat price—especially with so many homes here featuring complicated, steep rooflines from the early 1900s. Tear-off and disposal should be listed as a separate line, specifying number of layers to remove and whether a dumpster is included. For underlayment, the quote must identify the product type (like synthetic or felt), with ice and water shield shown separately and its coverage at eaves detailed.

Because many Scranton and Old Forge houses need sheathing repairs, a contingency price for decking—stated per sheet—is non-negotiable. Be certain flashing, vent boots, and other penetrations are either itemized or clearly included. The estimate should break out any manufacturer warranty from the installation/workmanship warranty—these are not the same thing. Lastly, a proper payment schedule will require a small deposit (no more than 15%), with the balance due only once work is complete and inspected.

If a contractor gives you a lump-sum price with no square count or refuses to detail contingencies, you can’t compare their bid to anyone else’s. This is often a sign that surprises and change-orders will follow. Scrutiny always pays off here.

What insurance covers on a Lackawanna County roof

If your Lackawanna County roof suffers wind or hail damage, start by documenting everything before a contractor touches a shingle. Take clear, dated photos—both close up and of the full roof—and back them up. When you file a claim, your insurer will issue a written scope of loss, usually after sending an adjuster to look things over. This scope is not always complete; review it carefully before agreeing to any settlement.

If your policy is “replacement cost,” you’ll receive payment in two parts: first, the actual cash value (depreciated), and later, the balance after you show proof the roof was replaced. If it's “actual cash value” only, the payout will be lower and you’ll be responsible for the difference if you want a full-quality job.

Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires a written contract for any roofing work over $500, and that includes insurance-funded work. You have three business days to cancel once you receive the contract. Importantly, waiving a homeowner insurance deductible is prohibited by law—any contractor offering to “cover your deductible” is putting you at legal and financial risk. Walk away from anyone making that pitch, as it’s both illegal and a red flag for sloppy or fraudulent work.

In this market, most insurance adjusters expect to see quotes in the regional range of $9,000–$19,000 for a complete shingle roof, and you’ll need to prove damages match the payout. Insurers will not pay for pre-existing problems, so maintenance neglect will hurt your claim.

What Lackawanna County's climate does to a roof

Roofs in Lackawanna County face specific threats you won’t find just a few miles west or south. The Lackawanna Valley itself channels high winds up onto exposed ridgelines—especially in Scranton and Archbald—driving water beneath shingles and causing edge blow-offs. The regional ground snow load is 40 pounds per square foot, so every pitched roof needs underlayment that can handle persistent snow melt, not just occasional flurries.

Ice dams are a prime culprit. They form when snow melts off the warm roof deck above an unventilated attic, then freezes again at the colder eaves. This backs water up under shingles and through nail holes, soaking insulation and damaging ceilings. The result: repairs can jump from a simple patch at $475–$1,800 to a full roof and sheathing replacement at $9,000–$19,000 if you catch it late. For this region, code requires ice barrier underlayment—not just felt—for several feet from the eave line, because a standard underlayment will not stop damming.

Hail is less frequent here than in the Midwest, but when it does hit, it bruises the mat beneath the shingle granules. You may not see a hole, but the shingle will fail early—insurance often covers this under storm damage, so it’s worth having leaks or dimples inspected, especially on west-facing slopes. Finally, heavy ridge-top winds can find weaknesses at poorly flashed walls or chimneys, so solid metal flashing is nonnegotiable and cuts down on repeated repairs.

The scope items Lackawanna County homeowners don't budget for

In Lackawanna County—especially with the older row and duplex houses in Scranton and Old Forge—the initial roofing quote often grows once tear-off starts. The biggest surprise is rotted or delaminated wood decking, hidden beneath multiple past roofs. Replacing plank sheathing or plywood runs $85–$140 per sheet, easily adding $800–$2,500 if several sections are compromised.

Another unbudgeted item: attic ventilation. In our cold climate, poor airflow behind the roof deck lets interior heat melt snow, fueling ice dams that lead to leaks and shingle damage. Upgrades—like ridge vent, box vents, or soffit work—can add $700–$1,900 but are necessary to back up your warranty and stop recurrence.

Flashing replacement at chimneys and siding transitions is also a frequent “extra.” Bad or rusted flashing can make otherwise good shingles leak and isn't always spotted from street level. Full chimney and wall flashing packages usually run $600–$1,400 depending on detail and size. Don’t forget vent boots and drip edge—if they’re not on your quote, expect a change order for $100–$400 per detail.

Ridge vent installation should not be considered optional. The cost is modest in comparison—typically $400–$850—but without it, even new shingles could fail early due to trapped attic moisture.

Low-slope and flat roofs in Lackawanna County

A surprising number of Lackawanna County homes—especially porch roofs, rear additions, and the majority of rowhouses—have at least one low-slope or flat section. These areas cannot use architectural shingles reliably: on slopes below 3/12, water moves too slowly and will work under the shingles, leading to chronic leaks, rot, and costly repairs.

For flat surfaces, modern single-ply membranes like TPO or EPDM are the standard. These create a continuous, watertight surface designed for ponding water that won’t run off quickly. They’re dramatically longer-lived on flat decks than even the best shingle at a similar price point. Membrane systems in Lackawanna County last about 15–25 years and full replacement ranges $11,500–$22,500 for typical flat roofs, depending on complexity and access.

If a roofer wants to use shingles on a 2/12 pitch in this county, that’s a clear signal they either don’t know local code or don’t care. Don’t hire them. Proper flat-roof work should also include attention to ponding areas—low spots require built-up substrate and possibly added drains, not just “more caulk.”

When a Lackawanna County roof is worth repairing — and when it isn't

Not every leak or missing shingle means you need a whole new roof in Lackawanna County. If your roof is under 15 years old and you find one isolated leak or damage—such as a blown-off tab or puncture from a tree branch—a focused repair at $475–$1,800 is usually the right move. This is especially true on the single-family stock here, where repair can buy another decade.

But with so many homes built before 1980, the odds rise that multiple leak points signal a system at the end of its life. If you’re doing repairs every winter, or are already patching leaks on a roof 20–25 years old, a replacement at $9,000–$19,000 becomes more cost-effective than cycling through constant call-outs. For example, three rounds of $1,200 repairs in as many seasons already puts you in replacement territory—without solving the underlying issues.

Storm damage is different: insurance often covers full replacement, especially for wind or hail events common on ridgeline homes in Scranton and Archbald. Flat roofs are another story—if ponding or blisters are obvious, replacing just the membrane for $11,500–$22,500 is nearly always preferable to spot repairs. Always prioritize emergency repair before winter to avoid extensive ceiling and insulation damage.

Roofing permits in Lackawanna County: what you need before work starts

In Lackawanna County, all roofing work is subject to the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), which uses the International Residential Code as its basis. Scranton enforces this code through the City of Scranton Department of Licensing, Inspections & Permits. In Scranton, any full tear-off and replacement on a one- to four-family dwelling requires a building permit before work begins. For small, like-for-like shingle repairs, both Scranton and Dunmore do not typically require a permit as long as the work is limited in scope.

Contractors must have a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration issued by the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General for any contract over $500. Homeowners can verify that a roofer is registered by checking hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Proof of workers’ compensation insurance is required for all employers, and this can be checked by looking up the contractor on the WCAIS system at wcais.pa.gov. Always ask for both registration and insurance certificates before signing anything—a reputable local contractor will have both handy.

Do not proceed with any roofing job over $500 without a written contract meeting state standards; Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act gives you the right to three business days to cancel after signing.

Is ice barrier underlayment required in Lackawanna County? Yes. The Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code, enforced by Scranton’s licensing and inspections department, mandates ice and water shield underlayment at the eaves because of our winter design temperature (4°F) and ground snow load. A legitimate roofing quote here will always show ice barrier as a separate item, and skipping it is a code violation that puts your roof at risk. Trustworthy local contractors in Lackawanna County know this rule and will not make such offers. What causes ice dams on Lackawanna County roofs? Ice dams here are driven by attic heat escaping through unvented or poorly insulated spaces—common with the early-1900s housing stock in Scranton and Old Forge. Warm air melts the underside of rooftop snow, which runs down to the cold eaves and freezes, creating a dam. Water then pools behind the ice and seeps up under shingles, causing leaks and soffit or ceiling damage. Repairs for resulting leaks average $475–$1,800, but if ignored, damage may require a full re-roof. Ensuring solid attic ventilation and ice barrier underlayment is non-negotiable in our winter climate.
What happens if rotted decking is found after tear-off? This is common in Lackawanna County’s older wood-framed homes. When shingles and felt are removed, contractors often find sections of soft or delaminated wood from years of leaks or trapped moisture. Normally, your contract should include a per-sheet price for these repairs—usually $85–$140 per 4x8 sheet. Spot repairs for one or two sheets can run $300–$800, but full deck replacement adds more. How many quotes should I get for a roof in Lackawanna County? Can I install new shingles over old ones in Pennsylvania? Pennsylvania code allows for a second layer of shingles in some cases, but in Lackawanna County, most older roofs already have at least one layer beneath, and inspectors in Scranton may restrict this for heavy, steep, or historic structures. While metal systems do cost more (expect $21,500–$39,000 for most county homes), noise is not a valid reason to avoid them. Will filing a roof claim raise my premium? Insurance companies sometimes impose a surcharge or drop wind/hail coverage in high-claim ZIPs, but if the event was widespread, increases may be universal, not specific to your home. For major claims (such as a roof replacement in the $9,000–$19,000 range), weigh the benefit against any long-term premium impact. If in doubt, ask your agent how your insurer views local storm claims.
