Roofing Replacement & Repair costs in Blair County — 2026

Blair County’s roofing replacement and repair costs reflect the realities of its distinctive housing stock and local labor market. With 27,260 owner-occupied households and nearly 67% of homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), roofers here are no strangers to the complications of aged plank decking and layered renovations on homes in Altoona and Hollidaysburg. When you replace a roof on a 1900–1940 wood-frame house—the backbone of Altoona and Hollidaysburg neighborhoods—the job nearly always requires assessing for soft or uneven plank decking under the old roof. This drives up the labor time and means replacement pricing in Blair County needs to allow for more deck repair and disposal than you might find in newer subdivisions elsewhere.

Combining this with the area’s moderate contractor density, competitive bids still sit solidly within predictable bands. But don't expect a cheap shingle job if your home is one of the tens of thousands built pre-World War II. Especially in denser Pleasant Valley sections of Altoona, recurring replacement cycles on older structures mean roofers must budget for hidden carpentry. TPO and EPDM on Blair County homes

Many homes in Blair County were built with flat or low-slope sections—rear additions, porches, and sometimes entire rowhouses—especially in Altoona and Hollidaysburg’s denser blocks. These sections need a very different roofing system than a standard slope roof. Traditional shingles are not designed for slopes below roughly 3:12; putting shingles on a low pitch (especially 2:12 or less) almost always leads to early leaks. Water travels more slowly on these slopes, so even a small dip or seam misalignment allows ponding and seepage below the surface.

A single-ply membrane—either TPO or EPDM—is designed to handle the near-flat geometry common to Blair County’s porch roofs. These membranes are heat-welded (TPO) or adhered (EPDM) in sheets that minimize seams, meaning water simply can’t find the same entry points. A properly installed flat membrane roof will typically last 15–25 years, compared to half that, or less, for shingles poorly applied on flat areas. On average, replacing a flat roof membrane in Blair County costs between $11,500–$23,000, a significant but necessary investment if you don’t want to redo it every handful of winters.

If a roofer suggests installing shingles on a flat or very low-pitch roof, that’s a warning sign—they’re either ignoring the standards or hoping you won’t notice. For flat areas on Blair County homes, always demand the right membrane system, even if it doesn’t match the look of the main roof from the street.

Snow load, ice dams and wind in Blair County

South-central Pennsylvania’s ground snow load peaks in Blair County compared to most of the state—40 pounds per square foot is the code baseline here—so the weight snow adds to your rafters is not not theoretical. The winter design temperature sits at 2 °F, with a heating season stretching longer than most counties around us. More important for roofers, Altoona and Frankstown Township sit along Brush Mountain and Canoe Mountain, zones known for ridge-line winds that can peel shingles and test the limits of a weak fastening job.

Ice dams are the silent budget-killer for Blair County homeowners. When attic heat melts snow high on your roof, the runoff refreezes at the cold eave—right above the gutter—forming a ridge of ice. Water backs up under the shingles, damaging your wood deck and often staining interior walls. This is why Blair County homes, especially those with north-facing slopes or poor attic ventilation, need wider ice-and-water shield protection than what’s used a county or two south. Replacing the eave area—because underlayment failed—is a real cost here, and even just repairing the damage from a single bad ice dam can run $475–$1,800.

Hail is less common but it leaves its own signature: shingles can be bruised, with the granules knocked away, weakening their weather seal. The damage might not look dramatic at first, but it cuts years off a roof's life and usually voids the manufacturer warranty. Over time, these impacts add up to premature full replacement—well before you’d expect, which is why some homeowners invest in impact-resistant shingles, spending $15,000–$19,000 for the upgrade. For wind-exposed sites near mountain gaps, architectural shingles or a switch to metal can help protect against those high-velocity gusts.

Water management on a Blair County roof

A new roof in Blair County isn’t a one-line solve: your drainage system is only as good as the sum of gutters, downspouts, drip edge, and even ground grading around the foundation. With our long heating season (over 6,000 degree days per year) and frequent freeze/thaw cycles, clogged or undersized gutters do more than overflow—they can drive water toward the base of old stone and brick foundations, quickly leading to basement seepage or worse.

A roof replacement that skips over rusty or undersized gutters is a partial fix at best. Modern warranties, especially on asphalt and metal systems, may hinge on having functional gutters and proper overhangs to ensure water is efficiently diverted off the roof deck and away from the home. Gutter replacement alongside a roof job in Blair County typically runs between $1,100–$2,800 for a standard single-family home, with guards a sensible upgrade if you have overhanging trees.

Don’t overlook downspout discharge: water dumping just a foot from your wall is asking for foundation trouble—grading and solid extensions are sometimes a smarter spend than adding more pipe. A comprehensive approach may cost a bit more up front, but saves on repeated service calls and keeps the whole home system dry, even when the mountain snow starts to melt.

When a Blair County roof is worth repairing — and when it isn't

Blair County’s older housing stock means repair versus replacement is not just about what you “can” patch, but about what makes sense given the age and type of roof deck you’re dealing with. If a single leak turns up on a roof under 15 years old—especially on late-model architectural shingles—a straightforward repair in the $475–$1,800 range is a smart spend. But repeated leak calls on a 20+ year-old shingle roof, especially in the 1900–1940 homes all over Altoona and Hollidaysburg, can waste thousands chasing new trouble every season.

If you’re paying for two or more significant repairs within five years, that’s money adding up fast—say, two repairs at $1,150 each makes $2,300 sunk into an old roof, with no guarantee you won’t end up replacing it anyway for $15,000 soon after. Severe wind or hail damage, commonplace near our ridges, is also a hard stop; insurance claims are built for full replacement, not for chasing partial repairs that will leave you exposed at the next storm. Flat roofs with ponding don’t improve with spot repairs; once you see major bubbling or leaks, the only real answer is full membrane replacement, usually $11,500–$23,000.

Use this as a local rule of thumb: if your roof is nearing or past 25 years and leaks, replacement is nearly always less risky for your budget and home value than another patch.

What a proper Blair County roofing quote contains

Any honest roofing contractor in Blair County should present you with a written quote where totals are broken down, not just a lump sum. The quote should state the roof area in “squares” (100 square feet each), account for the actual pitch, and spell out how many layers of your old roof will be removed. Underlayment (and especially ice-and-water shield) products should be called out by brand or spec—“felt” alone isn’t adequate for our snow zone. Since so many houses in Altoona and Hollidaysburg have plank decking and hidden rot, a real quote will include a per-sheet price for deck replacement as a contingency.

Look for separate line items for flashing, vent boots or pipe collars, and don’t settle for “Warranty: 30 Years” if that’s only the material rating—a proper contract will show a separate workmanship warranty in plain terms. Payments? Never pay more than 15% down in Blair County; balance should always be due on completion. If square count, tear-off, or deck work is missing or one figure covers everything, you can't do any apples-to-apples comparison and may be missing big scope items.

How to vet a Blair County roofing contractor

Hiring a roofer in Blair County is never blind trust: you can check every necessary credential. Pennsylvania requires every contractor doing over $500 of home improvement work to hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, which you can verify at the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General’s website (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). Ask for their workers’ compensation insurance certificate—by law, all employers must carry coverage, and you can look up status quickly using WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov). For homes built before 1978, make sure the contractor has the proper EPA certification under the federal Lead RRP Rule; Blair’s prewar houses mean this isn’t optional.

  • Confirm HIC registration number at hic.attorneygeneral.gov
  • Check business address—should be local or regional, not a PO Box only
  • Request a copy of workers' compensation insurance; verify on wcais.pa.gov
  • Ask for an EPA Lead RRP certificate for pre-1978 homes
  • Review written contract—Pennsylvania law requires it
  • No promise or suggestion to waive your insurance deductible (illegal)
  • Itemized written proposal by square and scope
  • Check references for similar jobs in Altoona or Hollidaysburg
  • Verify crew is in-house or regular subcontractors, not “pickup” labor
  • No large cash deposit—should be 15% or less up front
  • Check manufacturer certification for specialty products (metal, membrane)
  • Look for responsive, in-person communication—not just text or web forms

Local red flags: door-knockers after storms (common in our moderate-density market); out-of-area or out-of-state vehicles with no local business address; no written contract; contractors refusing to provide their HIC registration or insurance documents; lump-sum bids with no breakdown by squares or scope.

Roofing permits in Blair County: what you need before work starts

Roofing projects in Blair County follow Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), which is based on the International Residential Code and enforced either by your specific municipality’s code office or by the PA Department of Labor & Industry for municipalities that have opted out. In Altoona, any structural roof replacement that involves changes to the sheathing or trusses typically requires a building permit—routine tear-off and replacement of shingles without deck work may not. In Logan Township, minor shingle repairs under one roofing square often proceed without permits, but check with the municipal code office before work begins.

For jobs over $500, your roofer must have Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, which you can confirm at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. All employers are required to carry workers’ compensation insurance; you can verify a contractor’s active insurance at wcais.pa.gov. Your contract must be in writing, with a 3-day right to cancel—both are protections under Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act.

If your home was built before 1978, federal Lead RRP Rule applies—verifiable at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov. Contractors may not offer to waive your homeowner’s deductible for insurance jobs. Before signing, ask for copies of all registrations and double-check them using the official registries above. Never rely solely on word of mouth or a business card.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in Blair County

Does attic ventilation matter in Blair County's climate? Attic ventilation is especially important in Blair County, given our long heating season (over 6,000 heating degree days annually) and high snow loads. Proper ventilation minimizes ice dam formation, which occurs when warm attic air melts roof snow that then refreezes at the eaves, leading to leaks under the shingles and into your decking. Many Altoona and Hollidaysburg homes were built well before modern venting strategies, so it’s common to find under-ventilated attics in these areas. If your roof leaks after snow, poor ventilation may be a root cause. Investing a bit more to correct attic vents—sometimes just $650–$1,200 extra on top of a $9,000–$19,000 roof replacement—can save thousands in interior damage and adds life to your new roof.
Why is my Blair County roofing quote higher than the online average? Many online estimates don’t account for Blair County’s unique mix of old plank decking and frequent need for carpentry repairs hidden under multiple layers of old roofing. A quote for a full replacement in Altoona or Hollidaysburg often comes in at $15,000–$19,000 for a standard architectural shingle job—which is often higher than online calculators. Much of that cost comes from more labor pulling up old materials and reinforcing aging decks (especially in homes built before 1940), plus disposal fees for all those old layers. Local labor rates may be lower than major metro areas, but the extra hours needed for prewar homes drive the price higher than generic web estimates.
Is a metal roof worth the extra cost in Blair County? A metal standing seam roof in Blair County is a substantial investment—$22,000–$39,000 —but it can be worth it in cases where you want maximum strength against wind uplift, long-term savings, and the lowest maintenance. This is especially appealing on homes exposed to the ridge-line winds near Brush and Canoe Mountains. Metal roofs also shed snow better, minimizing ice dams and leaks that plague older shingle roofs every winter. The upfront cost is higher than an asphalt replacement, but the 40–70 year lifespan (much longer than shingles) can pay off if you plan to stay in your home for decades or if the alternative is a deck replacement that pushes a shingle job toward the upper end of the price band anyway.
Can I install new shingles over old ones in Pennsylvania? Pennsylvania law allows for up to two layers of asphalt shingles, but Blair County’s prevalence of 1900–1940 homes complicates this. Most roofs in Altoona and Hollidaysburg have old plank decking, and adding another layer increases weight and hides deck damage—problems in our 40 psf snow load zone. While going over may shave $1,500–$2,500 off your immediate bill, it often leads to rot or leaks that cost more down the road. If you’re replacing the roof anyway, tearing off down to the decking—so the deck's condition can be inspected and repaired—is always the right call in Blair County.
What is the difference between replacement cost and actual cash value? In Blair County, most homeowner’s insurance policies offer either Replacement Cost Value (RCV) or Actual Cash Value (ACV) on roof claims. RCV covers the full cost of installing a new roof with similar materials—say, $15,000 for asphalt shingles. ACV deducts depreciation for age and wear; so a 25-year old roof might be valued at a fraction of new, leaving you to cover much of the replacement. Many Blair County homes are older, so the difference can mean thousands out of pocket on a claim. Always read your policy carefully and clarify with your insurer.
How do I verify a roofing contractor is registered in Pennsylvania? In Blair County and throughout Pennsylvania, contractors must have Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration for any job over $500. Before hiring, go to hic.attorneygeneral.gov and enter the company’s name or registration number—they’ll either show as active or not. For storm repair work that can run from $475–$1,800 up to a full replacement, this is your best defense against scams and unqualified operators. Also check that they carry current workers' compensation insurance, especially if a crew will be on your property.
How many quotes should I get for a roof in Blair County? In Blair County’s moderate-density market, it’s smart to get at least three written quotes—each specifying materials, squares, warranty, and terms—before committing. The spread on a new shingle roof can run $9,000–$19,000 depending on your exact decking and roofing system, and not all contractors factor in the same scope for hidden carpentry. Multiple quotes clarify what’s included (tear-off, deck replacement, disposal) and expose any contractor ignoring local code or material standards. Don’t pick only on price—trust, local experience, and references weigh just as heavily.
How do I file a storm damage roof claim in Pennsylvania? If you suspect storm damage on your Blair County home—missing shingles from wind, hail impact, or tree impact—document the damage with clear photos, then call your insurance company to open a claim. Get a local contractor with HIC registration (verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov) to provide a detailed inspection and written estimate, which can range from $475–$1,800 for repairs up to $19,000 for replacement. Insurance adjusters will visit, compare findings, and confirm scope. Never let any contractor begin major work before the adjuster’s visit or sign over your claim—they should work for you, not the contractor.
Is a sagging roof line an emergency? Yes—especially in Blair County’s older homes, a sudden sag or dip in a roofline can signal failed deck boards, broken rafters, or water-damaged structure. Given our local snow load of 40 psf, a weakened area might collapse under winter weight. A roofing contractor should assess immediately, and a temporary repair (like emergency tarping, costing $475–$1,800) should be done quickly until full replacement or framing repair is arranged—usually in the $9,000–$19,000 range, depending on the cause and extent. Never ignore sagging, especially in homes built before 1940.
Are impact-resistant shingles worth it in Blair County? For Blair County homes along ridge lines—where mountain winds and the occasional hailstorm are real threats—impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles offer longer service and potentially lower repair costs after storms. They cost more up front—jobs typically run $15,000–$19,000—but are built to withstand wind uplift and the minor debris impacts that wear down cheap shingles. On older homes or valuable properties, this upgrade can buy peace of mind and may be a requirement for some insurance policies in high-exposure areas of the county.
What happens if it rains mid-replacement? In Blair County, sudden rain during roofing is handled with emergency tarping and fast action, not wishful thinking. Any contractor worth hiring will have large tarps and plastic ready; the crew should cover all open sections quickly, minimizing water reaching the deck or interior. Typically, this will not add to your final bill unless a true storm flood delays everything or causes interior work (which would be a rare claim). Emergency tarping is factored into repair and replacement price bands—$475–$1,800 for simple situations. Always ask your roofer how they handle weather delays before signing the contract.
