Roofing warranties in North Union: what's actually covered

Many North Union homeowners get burned because they don’t realize there are two different warranties when it comes to their roof. The first is the manufacturer’s warranty—this only covers you for material defects, not how the shingles were installed. These warranties are almost always prorated, meaning the payout drops over time. If you want coverage to transfer to a new owner, you have to follow the registration rules: most manufacturers require paperwork to be filed within 60 days of completion, and a resale usually limits the next owner’s coverage window.

The second warranty comes from your roofing contractor, covering their installation (workmanship). In North Union, you’ll see 5-, 10-, even 25-year workmanship warranties from contractors big and small. But a long warranty means nothing if the business folds—which can happen fast in Fayette County with smaller, traveling crews. A 25-year promise from a two-year-old operation is less reliable than a 10-year warranty from a contractor with decades of local history.

Both warranty types can be voided if your attic ventilation is under-code or if shingles are installed over existing layers—two problems you’ll see often on older Uniontown-era homes. Always ask for both warranties in writing, check transfer rules, and don’t skip registration.

Reading a roofing estimate line by line

A roofing estimate in North Union should be crystal clear—as old homes hide enough surprises without vague paperwork adding to the mix. Insist every quote states the number of “squares” (100 sq. ft. of roofing) and roof pitch outright. Tear-off and disposal should be itemized by the number of layers, showing if a dumpster is included or just haul-away. Look for named underlayment types (not just “felt”), a specific ice and water barrier coverage height, and a contingency price for decking repairs, because so many 1900–1940 houses need it once the old roof comes off.

The estimate should spell out what warranty is included—separating the manufacturer warranty from the contractor’s own (many try to blur these). The payment schedule matters: in Fayette County, never agree to a big upfront payment. Above all, lump sums with no square count or vague “includes all materials” blur are a red flag—those hide details you’ll pay for later and make it impossible to compare quotes apples to apples.

Storm damage and insurance claims in North Union

If your North Union roof is hit by hail or wind, start by taking dated photos and documenting the damage before making any temporary repairs. Save samples of shingles or flashing if they were blown off. Next, file a claim with your insurer to get an adjuster assigned—don’t let a roofer pressure you into starting work until you have a written scope of loss from the insurance company.

Most homeowner policies here pay actual cash value (ACV) first, then replacement cost only after work is done. That means your first check will be less than the full repair value. Get estimates from roofers for the full job (not just a “patch”) using only the written insurance scope so everyone’s working from the same baseline.

Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires a written contract for any roof work over $500, along with a three business-day right to cancel after you sign. Understand that waiving your insurance deductible is illegal—if a roofer offers to “eat your deductible,” walk away, because that’s insurance fraud by state law and will get your claim denied or you reported if it’s found out. Your best move in North Union is to use a roofer who understands how to bill in compliance with your insurer’s replacement cost requirements, and only pay your own deductible out of pocket—the law here is clear.

Decking, ventilation and flashing — the hidden roof costs

In North Union, because so much housing dates to 1900–1940, hidden costs on roofing projects almost always show up as rotted or pieced-in wood decking under old shingles. A typical contingency in your quote covers replacing 3–6 sheets of ½-inch OSB or plywood at $75–$120 per sheet. If soft spots or “bounce” is felt during the walkover, you should expect to need at least this allowance.

Attic ventilation is not an afterthought in Fayette County’s climate. With ground snow loads and freeze-thaw cycles, poor airflow—especially with blocked ridge or soffit vents—leads directly to ice damming, which lifts shingles and soaks insulation. Remedying it may add $600–$1,800 if new ridge vent or additional intake vents are needed. Flashing at brick chimneys or walls often needs to be rebuilt or replaced in these older homes, running $300–$750 per area. Cracked vent boots or missing drip edge (not included by default in many rural quotes) should add $75–$225 per boot/run. These costs aren’t padding—they’re common finds, not gotchas, on North Union tear-offs.

Permits and code requirements in North Union

Roofing work in North Union Township is governed by the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), based on the IRC. Code is enforced by each municipality or, in certain areas, by the PA Department of Labor & Industry if your township opted out. In Uniontown proper, a permit is needed for replacing roof covering over more than one dwelling unit—but in the rural Fayette County townships, minor shingle patching on a single-family home usually doesn’t require a permit.

Before hiring a contractor, verify their Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Confirm that workers’ compensation insurance is current and valid by checking the WCAIS system at wcais.pa.gov. For homes built before 1978, check for EPA Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) certification at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov. Pennsylvania has no roofing trade license, so do not be misled by claims of “state-licensed roofer.”

All contracts over $500 must be written, with a three-day right to cancel. If a contractor resists giving documentation or proof through these state registries, find someone else.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in North Union

Does homeowner's insurance cover roof replacement in North Union?

In North Union, homeowner’s insurance typically only covers roof replacement when the damage is sudden and accidental—such as wind tearing shingles off, falling trees, or hail. Age, general wear, or leaks from old materials are not covered. You’ll have to prove the date and cause with photos and insurance forms. Insurers here usually pay actual cash value (ACV) first, then reimburse the replacement cost after work is verified. Typical claim-paid roof replacements for storm damage in Uniontown and rural Fayette County fall in the $8,400–$17,500 range for asphalt shingles. Don’t get tripped up by roofers who offer to “waive your deductible”—that’s actually prohibited by Pennsylvania law and can risk getting your claim denied. Always insist the roofer works directly from your insurer’s written scope of damage, not just their own invoice.

What are the signs of hail damage on a roof?

Hail damage on North Union roofs, especially on the older 30- to 100-year-old houses common here, can show as random, round black “bruises” where granules are knocked off asphalt shingles. Shingles may look dimpled or have soft spots you can feel when pressed. Metal vent covers or flashing may show obvious dings. If your house sits on the Laurel Highlands side, you’re also susceptible to wind-driven hail stripping off tabs or shattering brittle old fiberglass matting. North Union roof repairs after hail run $450–$1,650 for local patching, but full replacements happen when coverage is widespread. Document any suspected hail impact right after the storm—older roofs blend damage with old wear, and adjusters will scrutinize every mark.

What is the difference between TPO and EPDM?

TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) and EPDM (ethylene propylene diene monomer) are both used for flat roof replacements on Uniontown-area additions or low-slope porches. EPDM is a rubber membrane, black and flexible, better for resisting ponding and ideal in areas shaded on the north side (where growth can be an issue). TPO is white, reflects sunlight, and is often chosen when energy efficiency or visibility matters, such as on newer flat-roofed entries. Both run $10,500–$21,000 for a full replacement in North Union. Local suppliers may lean toward TPO for stock, but either option will meet code and survive the freeze-thaw cycles if installed right. Choose based on color, warranty length, and installer preference—not because one is “always better.”

How much does a new roof cost in North Union?

For a typical single-family home in North Union, a new architectural asphalt shingle roof, completely stripped and installed, falls between $8,400–$17,500 in 2026. That band covers most older homes in the area, especially those built before 1980 with several layers to remove or plywood decking repairs. Metal standing seam roofs cost more—think $20,500–$36,000—but are rare due to price and what’s locally stocked. Always make sure the quote includes tear-off, disposal, and a “per sheet” decking allowance, since roofs in this area often hide surprises that old estimates miss.

What should a roofing quote include in North Union?

In North Union, a roofing quote should specify the number of “squares” (each 100 square feet), the roof pitch, and the number of shingle layers to be removed. Key items are dumpster/disposal, type and brand of underlayment, ice-and-water barrier at the eaves, and a separate per-sheet cost for any replacement decking. The quote must list the manufacturer AND workmanship warranties, as they cover different things. No estimate should be a “lump sum” alone—no way to compare scope or avoid upcharges later. Price bands here range between $8,400–$17,500 for typical tear-off and replacement, but every detail should be spelled out or you risk expensive surprises.

What does roof replacement cost per square in North Union?

A “square” is 100 square feet of roofing, and in North Union, the installed cost for architectural shingles in 2026 usually falls in the $350–$600 per square range, all inclusive. Variables include how many layers are removed, steepness, decking repair are needed, and if you’re close to supplier lines or far out in rural zones that tack on travel surcharges. For an average 30-square roof, most homeowners pay between $10,500–$17,500. Don’t compare square pricing alone without seeing whether the quote covers deck replacement, chimney flashing, or permits—that’s how old-house projects go from “lowest bid” to big arguments midway.

Does attic ventilation matter in North Union's climate?

Yes—attic ventilation is vital for North Union homes because winter freeze-thaw cycles and a ground snow load of 30 psf make ice damming a real risk. Poor ventilation means warm air melts snow, water backs up under shingles, and leaks follow—especially in older houses built with minimal soffit ventilation. Upgrading or correcting ridge vent, soffits, or attic fans can add $600–$1,800 to your project but will preserve your shingle warranty and keep utility costs down. Improper ventilation is a common reason for roof failures (and denied warranty claims) in Fayette County homes built before 1980, given their age and original construction.

Can I install new shingles over old ones in Pennsylvania?

In Pennsylvania, roofing codes—like the Uniform Construction Code—allow one layer of new shingles over an existing layer in some circumstances. However, in North Union, most homes built before 1980 already have two or more layers, and most reputable contractors recommend a full tear-off because hidden decking issues are so common here. Layering can void manufacturer warranties and may violate code if the decking is soft or the roof is already layered. Even if it’s technically permitted in a rural township, deck repairs can’t be done under leave-on jobs, and insurance claims may get denied. A true re-roof (with tear-off) runs $8,400–$17,500 for a typical North Union shingle home, and skipping it can cost more down the line.

What happens if rotted decking is found after tear-off?

In North Union, it’s common for roofers to discover rotted or broken decking once the old shingles are off—especially on homes from 1900 to 1940 built with wide-plank or pieced-in lumber. When this happens, your contract should include an agreed “per sheet” charge for replacing plywood or OSB, usually between $75–$120 per sheet labor and material. The roofer will show you the damage before replacing anything and document it for your records. Reputable contractors discuss deck repair as a standard part of the estimate, not a surprise change order. Make sure your quote spells out this allowance and how overage will be handled, since skipping needed replacement guarantees leaks and failed shingles.
