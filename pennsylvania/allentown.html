Roofing costs in Allentown reflect its older housing stock and mix of home types. The city is the county seat of Lehigh County, with 85.4% of Allentown homes built before 1980 and a homeownership rate of 42.3% (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024). Most single-family homes here have moderate roof complexity, with plenty of post-World War II tracts nearby in South Whitehall and Upper Macungie featuring near-identical asphalt shingle roofs. That uniformity means many re-roof jobs in Allentown are straightforward, limiting surprises and making for more consistent pricing.

When neighbors are all booking new roofs in the same decade, contractors streamline material sourcing and labor, keeping asphalt shingles competitive. While older neighborhoods might require carpentry or deck repairs, South Whitehall and Upper Macungie roofs are often simple rectangles—few cut angles, standard pitches, and vent locations. This economy of scale shows up in pricing and project duration.

How to vet a Allentown roofing contractor

Before hiring a roofing contractor in Allentown, check more than just a company name. Pennsylvania does not issue a roofing trade license, but every contractor working on residential projects over $500 must have a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, which you can directly verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Insurance is non-negotiable: workers’ compensation coverage is mandatory for all employers and should be cross-checked using the statewide WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). Because Allentown falls under municipal code enforcement, you can call the Bureau of Building Standards & Safety to verify necessary permits on your project. On pre-1978 homes, contractors disturbing paint must be certified under the EPA Lead RRP Rule—a detail you can confirm at the EPA’s renovator search database.

  • HIC registration confirmed online at hic.attorneygeneral.gov
  • Written quote provided, listing cost, scope and materials
  • Proof of active workers’ compensation (via wcais.pa.gov)
  • Certificate of liability insurance with your address as additional insured
  • Recent references from Allentown or Lehigh County jobs
  • Building permit application (for full replacement in city limits)
  • EPA Lead RRP certification for homes built before 1978
  • No claim or offer to waive your homeowner’s insurance deductible
  • Physical business address in the Allentown–Bethlehem area
  • Breakdown of brands and material weight in estimate
  • Project timeline in writing
  • Clear warranty documentation for both labor and materials

In a dense market like Lehigh County, watch for: storm-chasers canvassing after wind or hail events, companies pressuring for immediate signature “before the crews leave town,” vague or out-of-town addresses, or anyone without a valid HIC registration. If a quote sounds suspiciously low or requires cash-only payment, move on.

Asphalt, metal or membrane — what suits a Allentown roof

Allentown’s roofs face a ground snow load of 40 psf and winter design temperatures down to 4 °F. On most local single-family homes, architectural asphalt shingles are king: they’re affordable, widely available, and contractors here know how to install them fast and tight. That’s what’s installed on nearly every postwar tract in South Whitehall and Upper Macungie, because it handles the weight of snow and resists typical wind events without the budget hit of higher-end options. Metal roofing outlasts everything and sheds snow by design, which can be a long-term win for large, simple roofs with wide eave exposures—but the upfront cost is substantial. If your home isn’t set up for 50+ year ownership and you’re not on a tricky roof pitch, the added investment is rarely justified.

Flat roof systems (TPO or EPDM) are purpose-built for low-slope or flat areas—think downtown rowhomes or porch additions—not for sloped suburban homes. Their advantage is watertightness below 2:12 pitch, but they require careful installation because ponded water after a storm will test every seam. Asphalt’s dominance here comes down to cost-efficiency and skill: crews in Lehigh County install three to four times as many shingle systems as anything else, so they get the speed and performance dialed in.

Why gutters get replaced with the roof

A new roof in Allentown is only as dependable as the water management system it works with. Gutters, downspouts, and the roof’s drip edge need to be in sync: if the gutter is sagging, clogged, or undersized, runoff can overwhelm the foundation or erode landscaping, especially when winter snowmelt is channeled off the new roof. Downspouts discharging too close to your foundation raise the risk of interior basement seepage—a complaint on pre-1980 homes in the area. Code-compliant roof and gutter details are also a requirement for some shingle manufacturers’ warranties, and city inspectors can spot incompatible work during replacement.

Replacing gutters at the same time as your roof takes advantage of open access—no risking shingle edge damage or working around fresh installations months later. Most full gutter replacements (including removal and basic leaf guards) alongside a roof job in Allentown range from $1,800–$3,800 depending on home size and downspout runs. Upgrading discharge extensions and ensuring a proper drip edge at eaves can prevent headaches (and water issues) down the road.

Permits and code requirements in Allentown

Roofing in Allentown is regulated under the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), which is based on the IRC. The Bureau of Building Standards & Safety is responsible for code enforcement and issuing building permits within city limits. When is a permit required? In Allentown, any roof replacement that is structural in nature or involves adding new roofing over existing layers usually triggers a building permit. On the other hand, minor repairs—like spot-fixing a few shingles due to a leak—can generally be completed without a building permit.

All residential roofing contractors taking contracts over $500 must carry a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration from the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General; verify the number at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Workers’ compensation coverage is legally required for all employers, and Pennsylvania’s WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov) is your check for that coverage. On homes built before 1978, federal EPA Lead RRP Rule certification is needed for any contractor disturbing painted surfaces; confirm credentials at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov.

For any roofing project in Allentown, insist on a written contract (required under HICPA) and know your three-business-day right to cancel if you change your mind after signing.

Snow load, ice dams and wind in Allentown

Western Lehigh County, including Allentown, faces snow loads up to 40 pounds per square foot and winter design temperatures down to 4 °F. The topography—the ridges of South Mountain and Trexler Hills—funnels wind, creating local pockets of shingle blow-off and wind-driven rain infiltration. Most Allentown homeowners see the effects as ice dams along eaves: snow on a warm attic melts, refreezes at the cold soffit edge, and backs water up under shingles. With most homes built before 1980, original insulation and ventilation are often lacking, making this a regular repair driver.

A weak ice shield or skipped underlayment at eaves can lead to leaks that run behind siding and into the walls, resulting in sheetrock repairs and the need for roof deck patching. Fixing that sort of water entry can lead to charges in the $500–$1,900 range just to handle a local repair. Hail is less frequent but, when it crops up, it tends to bruise shingles—compressing and cracking the mat without puncturing the surface. Insurers know the risk and will sometimes push back on older, brittle roofs, especially if high-wind patterns match a known event corridor.

Proper underlayment (ice and water shield) at eaves and valleys is not optional here. It’s what separates a checkup from a major repair one snowstorm later. Contractors who routinely work in Allentown will factor these exposures into their material and labor recommendations—corner-cutters show up as repeat leak calls every winter.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in Allentown

Are storm-chasing roofers a problem in Allentown? Storm-chasing roofers do show up in Allentown, especially after wind or hail events funnel through areas like South Mountain and Trexler Hills. The dense Lehigh Valley market makes it easy for temporary crews to blend in and canvas neighborhoods just after a storm. These companies often pressure homeowners for quick signatures or low up-front costs, sometimes offering to "waive your deductible"—which is illegal under state law. You’ll also see a surge in yard signs and quick-turn quotes that don’t include code-required details. Homeowners should only deal with contractors who appear at hic.attorneygeneral.gov and provide physical references for other jobs in Lehigh County. Local repair costs for shingle blow-off after a storm typically run $500–$1,900.
Does attic ventilation matter in Allentown's climate? Yes, attic ventilation is crucial in Allentown due to its winter design temperature of 4 °F and frequent snow loads of up to 40 psf. Poor ventilation lets attic heat escape and melt snow unevenly on the roof, which is the prime driver of ice dam formation along the eaves. This is a recurring issue in Allentown’s older housing stock, where insulation and venting standards were lower before 1980. Improper venting can void certain shingle warranties and accelerate roof aging, especially if you’ve paid for Class 4 or architectural shingles in the $9,400–$20,000 range. Contractors should verify that vents are working and may suggest affordable upgrades to minimize ice dam risk.
Is a sagging roof line an emergency? A sagging roof line in Allentown—especially on pre-1980 homes—should always be checked by a professional quickly, as it may signal rotten decking or broken rafters aggravated by snow loads up to 40 psf. In some cases, historic layering (multiple shingle roofs on top of each other) can overload the structure. Immediate tarping or shoring may be needed to prevent water entry while a permanent repair is planned. Basic stabilization and patch repairs for a structurally compromised area can start at $500–$1,900, but full deck or rafter replacement will be much higher. Delay invites interior water and mold damage.
What underlayment should be used on a Allentown roof? On an Allentown roof, it is standard and wise to use an ice and water shield underlayment—especially on the first 3–6 feet from the eaves and in valleys—due to the region’s risk for ice dams and heavy snow accumulation. The Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC) calls for practices that match the environmental hazards of this market, and most manufacturers tie their extended warranties to proper underlayment. Skipping this to save a few hundred dollars in a roof replacement (typically $9,400–$20,000) often means facing leaks, repairs, and denied warranty claims in a single winter.
Do I need to leave my house during a roof replacement? Most Allentown homeowners do not need to leave their house during a roof replacement, unless you have special needs sensitive to noise, debris, or vibration. The process is noisy and a bit disruptive—rattling interior fixtures and dropping shingle bundles overhead—but reputable crews secure the jobsite to keep entrances clear. A typical tear-off and re-shingle on a standard Allentown home takes 1–3 days, depending on size and weather. If issues like deck rot are discovered, plan for an extra half day of work and cost closer to $15,500 total. Pets and small children are usually the largest concern.
Does homeowner's insurance cover roof replacement in Allentown? Homeowner’s insurance may cover roof replacement in Allentown if damage is from a covered event, such as wind, hail, or fallen tree limbs—conditions that occur in certain Lehigh County storm corridors. Insurance will not pay for roofs failing due to age or normal wear; those are the homeowner’s expense. Insurers in Pennsylvania require a detailed inspection, photos, and sometimes forensic review to confirm the cause. If your claim is accepted after a wind or hail event, the insurance check usually covers only Actual Cash Value unless you have Replacement Cost coverage. Expected out-of-pocket for a typical full replacement: $9,400–$20,000 unless insurance pays more.
How much does a new roof cost in Allentown? In Allentown, a complete architectural asphalt shingle roof replacement costs between $9,400–$20,000, with the average job falling near $15,500 for a typical home. Factors affecting the cost include roof size, steepness, number of layers to remove, and any carpentry or deck repairs required (especially common on homes built before 1980). Upgrading to impact-resistant shingles or metal roofing will increase the price, and flat roof systems land in between. Always request a detailed quote specifying materials—cheaper, poorly installed roofs rarely hold up to Lehigh Valley winters.
Can I install new shingles over old ones in Pennsylvania? In Pennsylvania, and specifically Allentown, it is only permitted to install one more layer of shingles over an existing layer—but only if the roof structure is sound and there are no existing moisture or decking issues. In practice, many Allentown homes already have two layers, thanks to decades of quick overlays, so city code typically requires a full tear-off before a new roof can be installed. Overlaying can save about $1,000–$2,000 upfront, but risks voiding warranties and doesn’t fix deck rot or nail pops—common in homes built before 1980. Always ask your contractor to document roof layers before starting.
How much does a flat roof replacement cost in Allentown? Flat roof replacements in Allentown—using TPO or EPDM membrane—generally cost between $12,000–$23,500 for a standard rowhome or single-story addition. The price depends on roof size, number of penetrations, and whether old built-up roofing or insulation must be removed and replaced. Seams and flashing details are especially important due to our climate’s snow and freeze-thaw cycles. Expect the upper end of this range for jobs involving multiple skylights, complicated drainage, or structural deck repairs. Always verify the membrane thickness and warranty before signing, and be wary of bids that miss critical flashing upgrades in this market.
