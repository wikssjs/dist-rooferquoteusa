How to compare roofing quotes in Dunbar

In Dunbar, the quality of your roofing quote matters as much as the crew on the roof. Look for a quote that spells out the total number of roofing squares and describes the roof pitch—this is the basis for every other line item. Tear-off and disposal of old shingles should be listed per layer, with dumpster costs clear. Reliable roofers in Fayette County account for rotten or split decking by including a per-sheet price for replacement (most local roofs from 1900–1940 use plank decking that often needs work). Materials matter: the underlayment should be named by type and thickness, with ice barrier specified by coverage along the eaves—our winter design temp of 8°F means ice damming is real.

A trustworthy estimate breaks out the workmanship warranty apart from the shingle manufacturer's warranty, so you know who is on the hook for workmanship problems. Payment terms should limit upfront deposits (not more than 15%) and put the rest due on completion, in line with Pennsylvania's HICPA regulations. If your quote is one lump sum with no area—walk. There's no way to compare quality or scope, and no recourse if corners are cut.

What insurance covers on a Dunbar roof

If you face sudden roof damage in Dunbar—whether from Laurel Highlands winds or a fallen branch—start by photographing everything before tarping or cleanup. Insurance policies here in Fayette County respond only when damage is documented up front. Next, contact your insurer to open a claim; they’ll send an adjuster to write a scope of loss, which you should get in writing. This scope outlines what’s damaged, what will be covered, and whether you’ll be paid the roof’s actual cash value (depreciated for age) or the full replacement cost, depending on your policy.

It’s Pennsylvania law that contractors cannot offer to “cover” or absorb your deductible—this ban protects homeowners. Any contractor who suggests otherwise is violating the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act. If you’re offered this kind of deal, walk away: it’s a legal risk and often a sign of cut-rate or fraudulent work. Be aware of your three-business-day cancellation right when you sign a roofing contract greater than $500—a safeguard if you have doubts after meeting with your adjuster.

In Dunbar, insurance adjusters are familiar with the challenges of older houses and common local materials, but they’ll always check for evidence of long-term neglect. Clean “repair” patch jobs may not be covered if the roof is simply worn out, but direct storm or sudden impact damage is typically insurable if clearly documented.

Choosing a roofing material for Dunbar's climate

Dunbar sits in the heart of southwestern Pennsylvania's snow belt, with 30 pounds per square foot ground snow load and design winter lows of 8°F. Roofs here must cope with heavy freeze-thaw cycles and occasional high winds coming off the ridges. Asphalt architectural shingles dominate locally—not just because they’re cheap, but because supply yards stock them and experienced crews can install them efficiently on older, uneven roofs. These do fine on steep slopes but require a good ice and water barrier at eaves due to snow damming risk. Impact-rated Class 4 shingles are a step up where frequent wind or small hail is a problem, especially near the ridgeline.

Metal roofing (standing seam) beats anything else at shedding snow and surviving 40–70 years with little maintenance, which is why you’ll see it on better rural homes and on lower-slope roofs where ice damming is a constant battle. But many older homes in Dunbar simply weren’t designed for metal: original framing may require beefing up, and at more than $21,500–$38,500, it’s not often justified except on your forever house. Flat roofs—typical on porch additions—need single-ply TPO or EPDM; these will not match shingle or metal longevity, but they’re the only real solution for low pitches.

Pennsylvania code and Fayette County permit rules for roofing

Dunbar roofing work falls under the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), which is based on the IRC and enforced by each municipality, or by the PA Department of Labor & Industry if the local township has opted out. In nearby Uniontown, a building permit is required for roof replacement over more than one dwelling unit. For rural parts of Fayette County—like Bullskin Township—minor shingle patching on single-family homes rarely requires a permit. Most full tear-offs or structural repairs on any home will need approval from your local permit office.

Every Pennsylvania roofing contractor working on contracts over $500 must have Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, which you can verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. You should also confirm that the contractor carries current workers’ compensation coverage via the WCAIS database (wcais.pa.gov)—this is required by law for all employers in the state. Always insist on seeing both HIC registration and proof of insurance before any money changes hands.

Remember: Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires a written contract for any home roofing job over $500 and gives you a three-day right of cancellation. If your house was built before 1978, any disturbance of old painted surfaces requires EPA Lead RRP compliance, which you can check at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov.

Dunbar homes and their roof lines

Roofing contractors in Dunbar see a lot of the same challenges: about two-thirds of the local housing was built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), and many main roof decks are plank not plywood. That means every roof tear-off should plan for at least a few sheets of decking to be replaced, especially along the eaves where old ice dams have led to rot. The typical pitch on a 1930s–1950s home is steeper than the flatter lines you see in newer suburbs, originally meant to shed snow quickly.

Ice and water shield was not code when most Dunbar homes went up, so you’ll rarely find it under old shingles—if your attic’s insulation is thin, ice damming will be even worse. In Uniontown and Connellsville, and by extension Dunbar, two or sometimes even three layers of shingles are standard on older homes, since past repairs were done by overlay, not tear-off—this adds weight and can hide leaks. Plan for extra disposal and close inspection for soft decking. For the substantial pre-1940 stock, valleys and eaves need particular attention for flashing and ice dam protection.

When a Dunbar roof is worth repairing — and when it isn't

In Dunbar’s older neighborhoods, it’s easy to be tempted by patch jobs, especially when a repair quote lands between $475–$1,750. If your roof is less than 15 years old and there’s only one clear leak source—a slipped shingle, a cracked boot—patching is worth it. Once a roof nears 20 years, though, multiple leaks usually signal system failure: patching here means you’ll likely return for more repairs next year, and the spending adds up fast. For instance, three leak repairs over two years could run you about $3,300–$5,200—approaching half or more of a new asphalt roof (~$8,900–$18,500), but without the peace of mind or warranty.

If your roof is 25 years old or more and leaks, replacement isn’t just smart—it’s urgent. In the wake of a wind storm or hail, don’t just patch; document the loss and pursue insurance, since larger roof work aligns with Fayette County’s frequent storm exposure. Flat roofs with ponding or blistering membrane can rarely be economically saved—membrane replacement is the right call. Emergency repairs leading into winter are worth it just to buy time until decking can dry and a full replacement is practical.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in Dunbar

What happens if it rains mid-replacement? If a storm rolls in during your Dunbar roof replacement, a reputable contractor will have heavy-duty tarps on hand to tightly cover any exposed areas—this protection is critical for older homes with plank decking that can be ruined by even a brief soaking. Crews around Uniontown and Fayette County expect sudden weather, especially in spring and fall. Quality roofers will halt tear-off early if rain is forecast, focusing only on what can be securely dried in, and may return the next dry day to finish. The cost of emergency tarping, when needed as part of proper protection, is usually absorbed in the roof labor (unless left overnight), but stand-alone emergency service could add $475–$1,750 to your bill if not included. Most importantly: you shouldn't pay for water damage caused by contractor error. Ask your roofer about their rain plan and make sure it’s in writing before work begins.
What does it mean if my shingles are curling or losing granules? In Dunbar, curling or balding shingles are a sure sign your roof is nearing the end of its service life—especially common on the pre-1980 homes in our area. Asphalt shingles lose their granules from freeze-thaw cycles, constant sun, and the wind that whips off the Laurel Highlands. Curling happens when the shingle dries out or when it’s under-ventilated, which is frequent on older houses with dense attic framing. If you spot either issue, a small repair might buy you time if the roof is under 15 years old, which would run $475–$1,750. But once a roof gets into the 20–25 year range or older, full replacement, usually $8,900–$18,500, is the best route for Dunbar homes. Patching only delays the inevitable—and rot may be hidden beneath.
Why do roofs fail faster in southwestern Pennsylvania? Fayette County’s climate is hard on roofing. In Dunbar, the cycle of heavy snow, rapid thaw, wet spring, and summer sun rots older shingles more quickly than in milder parts of the state. The 30 psf ground snow load and deep winter lows (down to 8 °F) create ice-damming, pushing water up under old shingles—especially dangerous on almost any roof in Dunbar built before 1980. Wind from the ridgeline lifts shingles and blows in rain horizontally. Repair work may cover leaks temporarily for $475–$1,750, but the combined climate stress shortens the effective working life of most roofs compared to elsewhere in Pennsylvania. For maximum value, insist on ice and water shield at all vulnerable eaves and valleys—especially if you’re investing in a new roof at $8,900–$18,500 or more.
What if my insurer denies my roof claim? It’s not unusual for a Dunbar homeowner to have a roof claim denied—insurers around Fayette County often point to “wear and tear” if your roof is already on its last legs, especially on our many houses built before 1980. If you get a denial, read the adjuster’s written explanation carefully. Sometimes documentation is missing or the adjuster misread storm damage for neglect. You can appeal the decision, request a re-inspection, or hire a public adjuster (at your own cost). In most cases, though, if your roof is 20+ years old, insurers consider it too worn for “replacement cost” coverage. If so, the out-of-pocket cost for a new roof is usually $8,900–$18,500 for standard architectural shingles. For new roofs, check that your policy covers full value and keep all records for future storms.
How do I verify a roofing contractor is registered in Pennsylvania? Pennsylvania requires any roofing contractor working on jobs over $500 in Dunbar (or anywhere in the state) to carry Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration. You can verify a contractor instantly at hic.attorneygeneral.gov—the database is public and easy to use. Just type in the company’s name, and check that their registration is active and covers roofing. While you’re at it, ask for a certificate of workers’ compensation insurance, which you can also verify via the WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). Contractors who can’t produce this paperwork are breaking the law and should never start work on your home—especially when you’re about to spend $8,900–$18,500 or more on roofing. For your own protection, do not pay deposits over 15%.
What's the cheapest way to replace a roof in Dunbar? The lowest-cost way to replace a roof in Dunbar is with architectural asphalt shingles, installed by a crew with recent local references—but never skimp on code-minimum underlayment or skip tear-off on a multi-layered roof. Typical replacement with architectural shingles lands in the $8,900–$18,500 range, with costs on the low end for smaller, single-story houses common around here. Some homeowners are tempted by shingle overlays (adding a new layer over an old roof), but I don’t recommend this in Dunbar: older roofs here often hide rotten planks, and adding weight increases risk of sagging. Send your job out for at least two quotes with explicit line items so you know what’s included. Avoid corner cutting—you’ll pay more for leakage or premature replacement in a few years.
Why is my Dunbar roofing quote higher than the online average? Online averages rarely reflect what it actually costs to roof a house in Dunbar. Our local costs are higher because so many homes were built before 1980, meaning more rot, more tear-off labor, and discovery of hidden issues—from soft plank deck boards to multiple shingle layers. Trucks and crews also travel farther here, sometimes tacking on rural surcharges. Material supply houses in Fayette County mainly stock asphalt shingles, but prices reflect regional demand and supply limitations. Metal or TPO will run even more—up to $38,500 for large metal jobs. What underlayment should be used on a Dunbar roof? For Dunbar homes, underlayment choice matters. Because our climate brings freeze-thaw and ice damming, I recommend synthetic roofing underlayment for most re-roofs—stronger than old tar paper, it stands up to our windy hills and heavy snows. At least the first 3 feet from the eaves should get an ice and water barrier (rubberized membrane)—more if it’s a low slope or high-ice area. Decks built before 1980 often have gaps, so a good underlayment is cheap insurance. You’ll see this specified by product name and area covered in a thorough quote, and it’s well worth any little upcharge in your $8,900–$18,500 roof estimate. Refuse any proposal that just says “felt” or omits ice shield—those days are gone for Dunbar.

Ask neighbors who had work done in the last three years—Dunbar has a high rate of homeownership, so word of mouth matters here.
