TPO and EPDM on Lower Merion homes

Flat or low-slope roofs are common in Lower Merion—think porch roofs, kitchen additions, and rear sections on older homes. These areas demand a totally different roofing system than pitched main roofs. Asphalt shingles, which rely on water quickly running off, simply don’t last on slopes below 3/12: the water sneaks under tabs, and freeze/thaw cycles pull them right up. The result is leaks, rot, and repair call after repair call.

That’s why professional roofers here specify single-ply membranes like TPO or EPDM for any section at or under a 2/12 pitch. These materials are reinforced, flexible, and fully adhered or mechanically fastened for a watertight surface—built to resist ponding common on old porch and addition roofs in Lower Merion. A properly installed membrane lasts 15–25 years, which is only a bit shorter than a shingle roof, but the reliability and leak prevention are night and day. Expect a full flat roof membrane replacement in this area to cost $14,500–$29,000 depending on the square footage and whether additional insulation is included.

If a contractor quotes shingles on a 2/12 section, it tells you they’re either cutting corners or don’t know our local standards. That’s not a bid to trust with your house.

Choosing a roofer in Montgomery County

Montgomery County, including Lower Merion, is a crowded roofing market—there’s no shortage of available contractors, but that makes vetting each one even more important. In Pennsylvania, any job over $500 requires the contractor to carry a valid Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration from the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General (check it at hic.attorneygeneral.gov) and provide a written contract. Employers must have workers’ compensation coverage; check this at WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov).

  • Confirm HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov
  • Ask for a written contract—not a handshake agreement
  • Check workers’ compensation status via WCAIS
  • Insist on proof of liability insurance—get the actual certificate
  • Get and call at least two recent references, ideally in Montgomery County
  • Verify the actual business address (don’t accept a PO box only)
  • No contractor should offer to “take care of your deductible” (against state law)
  • For homes built before 1978: confirm EPA Lead RRP certification at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov
  • Ask about who will be physically working on your roof—are they employees or subcontractors?
  • Demand a clear, written proposal that spells out everything included (flashing, ventilation upgrades, number of layers torn off)
  • Be wary of pushy sales tactics or “today-only” pricing
  • Never pay large up-front deposits—one third is standard, not the whole job cost

Red flags specific to our saturated market: door-knockers showing up unannounced after a storm, companies without a real Montgomery County address, non-local area codes, and anyone pressing you to sign a contract on the spot “because they’re in the neighborhood.” If it feels rushed, keep looking.

Roof age and construction era in Lower Merion

Over 81% of Lower Merion homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), tying the township’s housing stock directly to mid-century subdivision and post-war tract development patterns common in surrounding Abington and Cheltenham. Roofs from that era are pitched (not flat), mostly 4/12 to 7/12, originally decked with 1x plank boards—plywood became standard only by the late 1970s. Few, if any, were built with a modern ice barrier: most only received basic asphalt felt underlayment, which means today’s replacements often expose hidden rot at the eaves where freeze-thaw is harshest.

Multiple roof layers are typical. Prior to the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), it was common practice to add new shingles over the old to save money. Many Lower Merion roofs now have two, sometimes even three layers stacked up, which can stress decking and shorten lifespan. Tearing off all old material is almost always necessary for a long-lasting replacement—and for code compliance.

With the first replacement typically needed 18–25 years in, most mid-century homes in Lower Merion are on their second or even third roof by now. Expect that the job will involve correcting past shortcuts: thick stacks of old shingles, fragile plank sheathing, and possible unvented attics that need updating to modern code during replacement.

Permits and code requirements in Lower Merion

In Lower Merion Township, roofing work that involves only minor repairs—specifically, fixing under one roofing square (100 square feet) of shingles—can usually be done without a permit. For anything more substantial, including replacing over one square or performing a full roof replacement, you’ll need to pull a building permit. Permits in Lower Merion are enforced at the municipal level, under the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code (UCC), with oversight by the PA Department of Labor & Industry where townships have not opted in. In practice, Lower Merion Township operates its own code enforcement office.

To protect yourself, always verify that your roofing contractor is registered with the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General for Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) work (hic.attorneygeneral.gov). Workers’ compensation insurance is mandatory—every employer must carry it—and you can check compliance status at WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov). Ask to see certificates for liability insurance as well.

For homes built before 1978, the federal EPA Lead RRP Rule applies to any project disturbing painted surfaces; verify the renovator’s certification at certifiedrenovatorsearch.epa.gov. No statewide roofing or trade license is required in Pennsylvania, but HIC registration and written contracts for jobs over $500 are mandatory under the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act (HICPA). Remember—waiving your homeowner insurance deductible on a claim is illegal.

When a Lower Merion roof is worth repairing — and when it isn't

A repair seems tempting when your leak is minor and you’d like to avoid a complete replacement, but in Lower Merion’s aging housing stock, there’s a sharp line between a cost-effective fix and throwing money away. If your roof is under 15 years old and has one clear, isolated leak spot—for example, around a vent pipe or flashing—a professional repair costing $600–$2,300 can add years of service. But beyond 20 years, especially with multiple leaks, repairs become serial band-aids.

Take this scenario: if a 25-year-old roof develops several leak points, and you spend $1,450 each year patching it, three repairs put you over $4,350—money better invested in a replacement (typically $11,500–$24,500). Most Lower Merion roofs hit the 18–25 year threshold when even the best fixes are temporary. If your roof deck is original plank, and you see extensive shingle curling, patching rarely buys more than a season or two.

Flat roofing is even less forgiving: visible ponding, blistering, or seam failures on a 15-year-old membrane isn’t a repair job—it’s time for full replacement, generally $14,500–$29,000. Always ask for honest advice: if the reality is you’re due for a new roof, don’t pay a contractor to keep promising temporary miracles.

Timing a roof replacement in Lower Merion

In Lower Merion, timing your roof replacement pays—literally. The best months for new shingle installation are late April through mid-November, when temperatures average high enough for shingle seal-down and crews can work efficiently. Summer brings humid heat but is still good for roofing; the real price jump happens when you need a roof in winter. Replacing a roof during January or February, when temperatures drop into the teens overnight and snow is not uncommon (ground snow load: 30 psf), means special cold-weather techniques and higher labor costs.

Because Lower Merion is part of a saturated metro roofing market, top contractors can book out 6–10 weeks in peak months—sometimes longer if a major thunderstorm or tropical rain event moves through Montgomery County. By contrast, emergency tarping or midwinter jobs cost more and often can’t be scheduled except at premium pricing, since crews juggle fewer daylight hours and tough weather conditions.

If you want first pick of schedule and project team, start getting estimates six weeks ahead of your preferred install—especially for September and October, when everyone in Lower Merion hopes to replace roofs before winter hits.

Frequently asked questions — roofing in Lower Merion

How far ahead should I book a roofer in Lower Merion? For most Lower Merion roof replacements, especially during peak season (late summer to early fall), you’ll want to book your contractor at least 6 to 8 weeks in advance. The local roofing market is very saturated, and good crews fill their schedules quickly, sometimes up to 10 weeks out after major storms roll through Montgomery County. If you’re planning a straightforward asphalt shingle replacement (priced between $11,500–$24,500), mid-summer and early autumn are the highest demand windows—aim to get quotes by July if you want work done by September. Emergency repairs can sometimes be performed within a week, but you’ll pay a premium for fast service, especially in winter. Bottom line: The sooner you schedule, the greater your choice of materials, crew, and timeline.
How can I tell if my roof decking is rotted? Telltale signs of roof deck rot in Lower Merion are pretty consistent with its pre-1980 construction era. If you’re seeing spongy areas underfoot in your attic—especially near eaves—or spotting dark stains and a musty smell, that’s likely rot in the old plank sheathing. Roofers often only discover the full scope after tearing off old shingles, which is common here because many houses have two or more roofing layers. If your roof is old and repairs keep surfacing in the same spot, ask your roofer if your contract includes a per-sheet charge for plywood replacement. For reference: an unexpected repair for a rotted roof deck during a full replacement can push your price from $11,500 to well above $19,000, depending on just how much wood needs swapping. A thorough inspection in the attic first is always worth it.
What's the cheapest way to replace a roof in Lower Merion? The minimum-cost, code-legal option for Lower Merion is a single-layer “architectural” asphalt shingle tear-off with basic underlayment and no structural repairs. This starts around $11,500, but that assumes no surprise deck rot and only simple roof shapes—big valleys, dormers, or replacing multiple layers will bump you up fast. Be wary of “lowest bid” contractors who push shingle overlays (which risk code trouble and poor longevity) or skip key steps like drip edge and proper attic ventilation. The real savings come from proper attic airflow and solid warranty coverage the first time—not by cutting corners. Always get a full, written contract and walk through exactly what’s included.
Can I install new shingles over old ones in Pennsylvania? Yes, Pennsylvania code allows you to install one new shingle layer over an existing one—provided the roof has only a single layer now and both the decking and old shingles are in solid shape. In practice, many Lower Merion homes already have two layers (if not three, from older, pre-code days), so another overlay is neither wise nor legal. Besides, overlays trap heat, hide problems, and usually void manufacturer warranties—especially important with architectural shingles costing $19,000 and up. For nearly all Lower Merion homes these days, full tear-off and replacement is the right call, both for code compliance and long-term value.
How do I file a storm damage roof claim in Pennsylvania? If your Lower Merion roof suffers wind, hail, or tree damage, start by thoroughly documenting everything: take clear photos, note the date and time, and secure any exposed areas with tarps. Let your homeowner’s insurance company know right away—Pennsylvania law requires a written contract from your contractor for jobs over $500. Never allow a contractor to “cover your deductible”; it’s illegal under the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act. After you file the claim, your insurer will usually send an adjuster to inspect. Using a reputable, locally registered roofer helps avoid claim delays and ensures work is properly documented for full roof replacements, which often run $11,500–$24,500 in this area.
Should I use a local roofer or a national company? Lower Merion benefits from a strong pool of local contractors thanks to its saturated roofing market. Local roofers usually have much more experience with Montgomery County codes and know what building inspectors expect. You’ll also get better service if issues crop up after installation—especially when your roof replacement runs $19,000 or more. National brands have their place, but crews may be from out-of-town and less familiar with the quirks of Lower Merion’s many pre-1980 homes. I recommend prioritizing contractors with a real local address, references in your township, and a commitment to standing by their work long after the check clears.
What is the best roofing material for Lower Merion's climate? For most Lower Merion homes, especially those built before 1980, architectural asphalt shingles offer the best reliability for the money, handling humid summers, moderate freeze-thaw cycles, and the occasional big storm. They offer a strong balance of cost (typically $11,500–$24,500 installed) and lifespan (25–30 years). If you want next-level durability or your roof is especially exposed, consider upgrading to Class 4 impact-resistant shingles or metal—but for the classic suburban home, shingles handle the weather and fit what local HOAs expect.
Is a metal roof worth the extra cost in Lower Merion? Metal standing seam roofs hold up exceptionally well in Lower Merion’s climate: they shed snow, resist winds off the Schuylkill valley, and shrug off our region’s humid summers. With lifespans of 40–70 years, a professionally installed metal roof (costing $28,000–$50,000) is a strong investment if you plan to stay in your house long-term or want to avoid ever replacing a roof again. For most Lower Merion homes, though, the premium is hard to justify on short-term ownership. Consider metal for high-end properties, tricky roof designs, or when energy efficiency is a top concern—it may also lower your insurance in select wind-prone pockets of the county.
Does Lower Merion get enough hail to justify Class 4 shingles? While Lower Merion doesn’t sit in Pennsylvania’s highest hail frequency zone, thunderstorms rolling up the Schuylkill River valley can still bring the occasional damaging hail or wind event. Most insurance premium reductions for Class 4 shingles (impact resistant, $19,000–$24,500) aren’t huge here, but if your home is surrounded by tall trees or is exposed to open wind corridors (like in parts of Upper Merion Township), Class 4 shingles could be a wise upgrade for roof longevity. On a typical block, though, standard architectural shingles remain the default, especially for 1950s–1970s vintage homes.
